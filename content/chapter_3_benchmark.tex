\chapter{Benchmarking single-cell chromatin accessibility sequencing methods}
\setcounter{figure}{1}  % Ensure the first figure starts with 1


The following chapter will describe how we benchmarked several of the most prominent scATAC-seq techniques available in 2023. We performed 47 experiments across 8 different scATAC-seq methods to generate 169k single-cell chromatin accessibility profiles. Our benchmark quantified reproducible technical biases in not only the sensitivity, but also the specificity of these methods, produced a scATAC-seq pre-processing pipeline with best practices tutorials, and yielded the largest dataset of scATAC-seq in PBMCs to date. This work was published in \fullcite{deropSystematicBenchmarkingSinglecell2023} (\autoref{fig:article_title_benchmark}, \autoref{app:benchmark}).

\section{Motivation and approach}
Throughout the development of HyDrop-ATAC, we were often confronted with questions surrounding the method's sensitivity (the number of fragments detected per cell) and specificity (the enrichment of these fragments in enhancers and near transcription start sites, collectively forming chromatin accessibility "peaks"). Such questions arose both internally and externally, and echoed previous calls for an in-depth method performance exploration since the release of Bio-Rad\supershortnotecite{lareauDropletbasedCombinatorialIndexing2019} and 10x Genomics'\supershortnotecite{satpathyMassivelyParallelSinglecell2019} scATAC-seq products in 2019. At the time, a number of open-source scATAC-seq protocols had also been published\sjcitep[,]{cusanovichsinglecellatlasvivo2018,chenRapidRobustMethod2018}, but required significant levels of expertise (e.g. automated liquid handling for scaling) or non-commercially available components (e.g. indexed Tn5). As a result, they remained in use exclusively at only a handful of labs. Both BioRad and 10x's scATAC-seq solutions were more user-friendly and handled the most technically challenging aspect, microfluidic droplet generation, on benchtop devices (10x's Chromium Controller and Bio-Rad's ddSEQ). However, the Chromium controller already enjoyed a wide presence in large research centres due to the success of 10x's scRNA-seq products, and the Fluidigm C1 had fallen out of use due to throughput and per-cell price considerations. As a result, 10x dominated the commercial scATAC-seq market, leading to [x] publications compared to ddSEQ's [y], and only approximately [z] publications generated with open-source methods by [date].

We reasoned that an exact quantification of the performance differences among commercial and non-commercial protocols could stimulate informed decision making. Two such benchmarks had been performed for scRNA-seq methods: one by Ziegenhain and Enard\sjcitep[,]{ziegenhainComparativeAnalysisSingleCell2017} and a larger, more recent study by Mereu and Heyn\sjcitep[.]{mereuBenchmarkingSinglecellRNAsequencing2020} In the Mereu benchmark, a reference sample was distributed to collaborating labs, which then each generated at least one scRNA-seq dataset. This reference consisted of a mixture of PBMCs, mouse colon cells, HEK293T, 3T3 and dog MDCK cells. This approach complicated genome alignment and further post-processing, and we therefore opted to limit the reference sample to a mixture of human PBMC extracted from one male and one female donor. The use of only liquid tissue also simplified sample handling: PBMC can be distributed frozen and thawed slowly before nuclei extraction. Finally, a large reference dataset of single-cell PBMC chromatin accessibility did not yet exist, ensuring that our benchmark's final dataset could also provide value as a public resource.

We included 7 different techniques in the initial phase of our benchmark. [ref to introduction] includes an overview of these methods from a mechanistic point of view.:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
	\item 10x Genomics scATAC-seq v1 and v1.1: The most widely used scATAC-seq method. While v1 reagent kits were technically still available for purchase, the microfluidic chip required for this kit had been decomissioned due to patent infringement litigation by BioRad. An updated version (v1.1) had been released together with the nextGEM chip, which circumvented the BioRad intellectual property by generating droplets in a reservoir deposition style instead of using flow focusing.
	\item 10x Multiome ATAC + Gene Expression: Introduced in [year], the 10x multiome kit generated both scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq data from the same cells. This novelty had previously only been demonstrated in plates [ref, doublecheck - when was scCAT-seq?]
	\item Bio-Rad ddSEQ SureCell ATAC: 10x's direct competitor. The Bio-Rad scATAC-seq method also used droplet microfluidics, but employed hard resin beads (like Drop-seq\sjcitep[]{macoskoHighlyParallelGenomewide2015}) and an ingenious overloading strategy to reach cell capture rates on par with 10x [ref introduction ddSEQ].
	\item mtscATAC-seq: A modification of the 10x scATAC-seq protocol where omission of Tween-20 and digitonin from nuclei extraction buffers keeps the cell membrane remain intact but permeabilized to Tn5. \section{Analysis}
Early on in the project, we foresaw that data stemming from the 8 different techniques would strongly differ in sequencing format. The 10x Genomics format set the standard, each fragment yielding two paired-end genomic reads, a sample index read, and a 16 basepair barcode read matching a whitelist containing ~2.7M entries. HyDrop was designed with a similar structure in mind, but with a barcode length of 50 bp. Bio-Rad and s3-ATAC used a very different logic: [] (\autoref{fig:library_types_and_sequencing_schematics}).

We had faced a similar problem during the development of HyDrop: despite the structural similarities between 10x Genomics' and HyDrop's sequencing format, 10x's CellRanger software could not be used to pre-process HyDrop data since several internal parameters (such as read lengths and barcode whitelist) were hard-coded to match 10x data. To solve this problem, Gert Hulselmans Christopher Flerin and I developed \verb|PUMATAC|, a \verb|Nextflow|\sjcitep[]{di_tommaso2017} pre-processing pipeline capable of aligning sequencing data agnostic of sequencing format (\autoref{fig:pumatac_workflow}). \verb|PUMATAC| takes raw sequencing data (\verb|.fastq|), trims adapters, performs barcode error correction using a user-specified whitelist, aligns genomic reads to a reference genome, and converts these genome alignments to \verb|.bed|-like \verb|.fragments| files. The use of \verb|Nextflow| provides several advantages:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
	\item Intelligent interruption procedures: per step in the pipeline, an [md5] hash of the sample names, input files, output files and parameters is computed. If the workflow is interrupted and re-started, these hashes are re-computed. If no change in the hash is detected, no conditions have changed, and the pipeline can resume where it left off. If a parameter is changed, the hash of relevant steps will change, and the pipeline will resume there.
	\item Management of parallel processing: samples and steps that are not dependent on each other can be scheduled independently from each other's completion status, facilitating co-processing of small and large datasets. Each step in the pipeline has definitions for maximum CPU and memory usage, and available resources are utilised maximally.
	\item Built-in support for software containers (docker, singularity), improving environment-independent compatibility.
\end{enumerate}

\verb|PUMATAC|-generated fragments files were then counted in ENCODE SCREEN regions using \verb|pycisTopic|\sjcitep[.]{bravogonzalezblascistopic2019} Cells were then filtered on fragment and TSS-enrichment cutoffs determined with Otsu's algorithm\sjcitep[.]{otsu1979} Once cell counts were known for all samples, data was downsampled to matching read depths and re-processed to account for differences in sequencing depths. Cells were then doublet-filtered (\verb|scrublet|)\sjcitep[]{wolock2019} assigned to donors (\verb|freemuxlet|)\sjcitep[]{zhang2019popscle} and cell types (\verb|seurat|)\sjcitep[.]{stuartcomprehensiveintegrationsinglecell2019} Cell-type specific peak sets were called (\verb|MACS2|)\sjcitep[]{zhangmodelbasedanalysischipseq2008} and and aggregated, and the entire process was repeated once more on these consensus peak sets. Finally, differentially accessible regions were called between cell types and donors, and motif enrichment in these DARs was performed using \verb|cisTarget|\sjcitep[.]{aibarscenicsinglecell2017}

\section{Key results and conclusion}
We found wide variability in "sequencing efficiency", the fraction of reads "used" compared to the total number of reads sequenced, between methods. During pre-processing, sequenced reads are filtered out of the dataset for various reasons: no valid barcode associated or bad genomic alignment (10-20\% of total), not within a peak region (5-15\%) etc, but the most significant losses are incurred when true cells are filtered from noise (7-60\%[[see also introd]]). FAC sorting live cells before nuclei extraction reduced this noise from 32\% to 7\% in mtscATAC-seq experiments, suggesting that much of it is caused by contaminating DNA from damaged cells. For all techniques, the number of "useful" sequencing reads was only a small fraction of the total number of reads sequenced, ranging between \num{28}\% for 10x v2 to below \num{4}\% for s3-ATAC and HyDrop v1. This fraction directly impacted overall sensitivity (absolute median number of fragments in peaks detected per cell) of these methods at a given sequencing depth (fig. 2a). In addition, the depth at which the number of unique fragments detected with respect to total number of reads sequenced begins to saturate varied greatly across methods (extended data fig. 10). These sensitivity differences directly affected all downstream analyises: doublet detection, genotype demultiplication, cell type annotation and significance of DARs all positively depend on median number of unique fragments detected per cell (fig. 2f-j), and these effects become more pronounced when investigating subtle differences (fig. 3j-k).

Fragment specificity was also distinct between methods in a number of ways. Immediately, fragment TSS enrichment and raw FRIP values differed strongly (fig. 1h). For example, Bio-Rad ddSEQ fragments were significantly more TSS-proximal than 10x fragments, which is reflected in the TSS-proximity of ddSEQ DARs (fig. 2j) and subsequent motif analyses (fig. 3g). In general, high-signal DARs were easily recovered by all techniques, but weak DARs were strongly technique-dependent (fig. 3e-f), implying technique-specific noise. This was also evident in how difficult samples integrated across technologies, even with computational batch-effect correction (fig. 4c-d). There are several potential sources of this noise. First of batch-dependent technical noise leads to quality correlations between technical replicates and center replicates (fig. 4c). Second, different template amplification are used: ddSEQ employs exponential in-droplet PCR, whereas 10x and HyDrop opt for a linear amplification regime. Finally, Tn5 sources, concentrations, buffers and tagmentation protocols differ across technologies. 

Awareness of these sensitivity and specificity biases is especially important in scATAC-seq studies since, as opposed to in (sc)RNA-seq, the count region vector is not portable between samples but rather a property of interest of the samples themselves. Therefore, high-specificity methods should be prioritised when determining an accurate reference region set if public references (e.g. ENCODE ChIP-seq or DNase-seq region sets) are not available a priori. Cost-efficient but less sensitive/specific open-source methods can then be used for data generation at scale. Sequencing efficiency of these open-source methods can also be improved by sequencing less deeply, since fewer reads will then be lost to duplicates. Naturally, per-cell sensitivity will suffer, but increasing the total number of cells may be able to partially compensate. For example, both cell type annotation quality (Seurat label transfer scores) and significance of differentially accessible regions increased with higher cell counts, though this effect had diminishing returns (fig. 3a). In the limit, global optima for the trade-offs between cell counts, per-cell depth, and total experimental cost with respect to maximum model predictive value remain an open problem, but our benchmark provides a strong evaluation framework for future datasets.
