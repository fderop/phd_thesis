\chapter{Benchmarking the performance of single-cell chromatin accessibility sequencing methods}
\setcounter{figure}{1}  % Set the figure counter to 1 already to include the first figure
The following chapter will describe how we benchmarked several of the most prominent scATAC-seq techniques available in 2023. We performed 47 experiments across 8 different scATAC-seq methods to generate 169k single-cell chromatin accessibility profiles. Our benchmark quantified reproducible technical biases in not only the sensitivity, but also the specificity of these methods, produced a scATAC-seq pre-processing pipeline with best practices tutorials, and yielded the largest dataset of scATAC-seq in PBMCs to date. This work was published in \fullcite{deropSystematicBenchmarkingSinglecell2023} (\autoref{fig:article_title_benchmark}).

\section{Motivation and approach}
Throughout the development of HyDrop-ATAC, we were often confronted with questions surrounding the method's sensitivity (the number of fragments detected per cell) and specificity (the enrichment of these fragments in enhancers and near transcription start sites, collectively forming chromatin accessibility "peaks"). Such questions arose both internally and externally, and echoed previous calls for an in-depth method performance exploration since the release of Bio-Rad\supershortnotecite{lareauDropletbasedCombinatorialIndexing2019} and 10x Genomics'\supershortnotecite{satpathyMassivelyParallelSinglecell2019} scATAC-seq products in 2019. At the time, a number of open-source scATAC-seq protocols had also been published\sjcitep[,]{chenRapidRobustMethod2018,mulqueenHighcontentSinglecellCombinatorial2021} [sci, name each of these techniques individually with ref, also mulqueen was not published yet!], but best-case data exhibited in these articles revealed that they could not compete with 10x in terms of raw per-cell sensitivity. Furthermore, most of these open-source protocols required significant levels of expertise (e.g. automated liquid handling) and/or non-commercially available components (e.g. indexed Tn5). As a result, they remained in use almost exclusively at only a handful of labs. Both BioRad and 10x's scATAC-seq solutions were more user-friendly and handled the most technically challenging aspect, microfluidic droplet generation, on benchtop devices (10x's Chromium Controller and Bio-Rad's ddSEQ). However, the Chromium controller already enjoyed a wide presence in large research centres due to the success of 10x's scRNA-seq products, and the Fluidigm C1 had fallen out of use due to throughput and per-cell price considerations. As a result, 10x dominated the commercial scATAC-seq market, leading to [x] publications compared to ddSEQ's [y], and only approximately [z] publications generated with open-source methods by [date].

We reasoned that an exact quantification of the performance differences among commercial and non-commercial protocols could stimulate informed decision making. Two such benchmarks had been performed for scRNA-seq methods: one by Ziegenhain and Enard\sjcitep[,]{ziegenhainComparativeAnalysisSingleCell2017} and a larger, more recent study by Mereu and Heyn\sjcitep[.]{mereuBenchmarkingSinglecellRNAsequencing2020} Both of these benchmarks employed the same strategy: a reference sample was distributed to collaborating labs [doublecheck for Ziegenhain], which each generated at least one scRNA-seq dataset. In the Mereu benchmark, the reference consisted of a mixture of [x] tissues across [y] species. This complicated genome alignment and further post-processing, and we therefore opted to limit the reference sample to a mixture of PBMC extracted from one male and one female donor. The use of a liquid tissue also simplified sample handling: PBMC can be distributed frozen, and only need to be thawed slowly before nuclei extraction can occur. In addition, a large reference dataset of single-cell PBMC chromatin accessibility did not yet exist.

We included 7 different techniques in the initial phase of our benchmark. [ref to introduction] includes an overview of these methods from a mechanistic point of view.:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
	\item 10x Genomics scATAC-seq v1 and v1.1: The most widely used scATAC-seq method. While v1 reagent kits were technically still available for purchase, the microfluidic chip required for this kit had been decomissioned due to patent infringement litigation by BioRad. An updated version (v1.1) had been released together with the nextGEM chip, which circumvented the BioRad intellectual property by generating droplets in a reservoir deposition style instead of using flow focusing.
	\item 10x Multiome ATAC + Gene Expression: Introduced in [year], the 10x multiome kit generated both scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq data from the same cells. This novelty had previously only been demonstrated in plates [ref, doublecheck - when was scCAT-seq?]
	\item Bio-Rad ddSEQ SureCell ATAC: 10x's direct competitor. The Bio-Rad scATAC-seq method also used droplet microfluidics, but employed hard resin beads (like Drop-seq\sjcitep[]{macoskoHighlyParallelGenomewide2015}) and an ingenious overloading strategy to reach cell capture rates on par with 10x [ref introduction ddSEQ].
	\item mtscATAC-seq: A modification of the 10x scATAC-seq protocol where omission of Tween-20 and digitonin from nuclei extraction buffers keeps the cell membrane remain intact but permeabilized to Tn5. As a result, the cell's mitochondria are retained, and their genomes are also sequenced, allowing lineage tracing.
	\item s3-ATAC: The most sensitive combinatorial indexing/plate-based assay to date. s3-ATAC uses uracil-intolerant polymerases to terminate extension on the [x] chain. , circumventing the traditional 50\% loss in sensitivity due to homotypic-ended fragments [is there a ref that describes this problem?].
	\item HyDrop: Our own in-house method. HyDrop's workflow is very similar to 10x scATAC-seq, but uses commercially available reagents and the Onyx microfluidics controller in combination with an open-source chip to encapsulate cells.
\end{itemize}

The 10x Genomics format set the standard, each fragment yielding two paired-end genomic reads, a sample index read, and a 16 basepair barcode read matching a whitelist containing ~2.7M entries. The use of NextFlow provides several advantages over shell scripts:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
	\item Intelligent interruption procedures: per step in the pipeline, an [md5] hash of the sample names, input files, output files and parameters is computed. If the workflow is interrupted and re-started, these hashes are re-computed. If no change in the hash is detected, no conditions have changed, and the pipeline can resume where it left off. If a parameter is changed, the hash of relevant steps will change, and the pipeline will resume there.
	\item Management of parallel processing: samples and steps that are not dependent on each other can be scheduled independently from each other's completion status, facilitating co-processing of small and large datasets. Each step in the pipeline has definitions for maximum CPU and memory usage, and available resources are utilised maximally.
	\item Built-in support for software containers (docker, singularity), improving environment-independent compatibility.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Key results}
We targeted a recovery of \textasciitilde{3000} cells for each dataset. The complete, filtered dataset spans \num{169227} cells, [[n reads]], across \num{198421} peaks.

One of our most surprising findings was the wide variability in "sequencing efficiency", the fraction of reads "used" compared to the total number of reads sequenced, between methods. During pre-processing, sequenced reads are filtered out of the dataset for various reasons. For example, no valid barcode associated or bad genomic alignment (10-20\% of total lost), not within a peak region (5-15\%) etc, but the most significant losses are incurred when true cells are filtered from noise (7-60\%[[see also introd]]). FAC sorting live cells before nuclei extraction reduced this noise from 32\% to 7\% in mtscATAC-seq experiments, suggesting that much of it is caused by contaminating DNA from damaged cells. Removal of duplicate reads also led to large losses, but this inefficiency can be mitigated by sequencing less. In this case, per-cell sensitivity suffers, but increasing the total number of cells may be able to compensate, at least partially. For example, both annotation quality (Seurat label transfer scores) and significance of differentially accessible regions scaled with higher cell counts. However, in the limit, global optima for the trade-off between cell-count, per-cell-sensitivity and cost remain an open question for the field [[ref to introd]].

Our study also confirmed that specificity is distinct between methods. For example, high-signal peaks are easily recovered by all techniques, but weak peaks are strongly technique-dependent (paper fig. 3e-f), implying process biases between methods. Process biases already lead to issues in scRNA-seq technologies, but are especially important to consider for scATAC-seq studies: the count-region vector for (sc)RNA-seq (genes) can be transported between samples, whereas these region sets are the properties of interest of the samples themselves. When public references (e.g. ENCODE ChIP-seq or DNase-seq region sets) are not available a priori, high-sensitivity methods should be used to accurately determine a reference region set, after which more cost-efficient methods in large cell volumes can be employed for scale.
