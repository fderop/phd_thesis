\chapter{Quantitative sequencing technologies}
\label{ch:quantitative}

\section{Sequencing assays}
Before the availability of next-generation sequencing technology, studying the genome and gene expression at scale relied heavily on microarrays. Microarrays were, and still are, used to quantify RNA transcripts\sjcitep[,]{schenaQuantitativeMonitoringGene1995} genotype samples using SNP arrays\sjcitep[,]{wang1998large} and even measure DNA-protein interactions using ChIP-on-chip\sjcitep[.]{ren2000genome} While microarrays allow for up to ~\num{50000} probes to be interrogated in a single experiment, limited dynamic range, high false-positive hybridisation and heterogeneous probe hybridisation capacities skew absolute quantification. The probe set also requires intelligent design and thus cannot discover novel sequences. Probeset size is particularly troubling for ChIP-on-Chip, since genome binning limits resolution to around 500-1000 bp - too low for novel TF motif discovery. Nonetheless, large scale, distributed application of microarray technologies culminated in a first draft of the Encyclopedia of DNA Elements (ENCODE) in 2007\sjcitep[,]{ENCODE_Project_Consortium2007-zd} a careful annotation of 30 MBs - around 1\% - of the human genome.

% The release of this draft was the last project of such scale that did not use next generation sequencing.

The now upcoming availability of NGS made it possible to bypass microarrays: specific fractions of nucleic acids extracted from tissues could be sequenced, aligned to the reference genome, and counted. This revolution led to an explosion of quantitative sequencing assays being developed. One of the first such assays was histone methylation ChIP-seq, first published in 2007\sjcitep[.]{barskiHighresolutionProfilingHistone2007} By sequencing immunoprecipitated DNA directly rather than using microarrays, methylation could now be assessed genome-wide and with fewer quantitative biases. Performing this technique for a spectrum of 20 different lys/arg histone methylation ChIP-seq variants on CD4\textsuperscript{+} T cells, and correlating the annotations with GNF SymAtlas gene expression microarray data allowed genome-wide classification and discovery of various classes of functional elements. The distribution of methylation signatures could now be systematically correlated to phenotypes, helping understand disease and development. For example, a comparison of histone methylation in mouse embryonic stem cells and embryonic fibroblasts revealed transitory methylation changes in the de-differentiation of ES\sjcitep[.]{mikkelsen2007genome}

In parallel research, ChIP-seq was also being applied to individual transcription factors at a genomic resolution of around 50 base pairs, a welcome improvement compared to microarrays. Application of ChIP-seq on neuron-restrictive silencer factor (NRSF) in Jurkat T-cells showed that the factor can also bind "weakly" to altered, non-canonical motifs dependent on local chromatin state (methylation, accessibility) and co-factor motif context\sjcitep[.]{johnsongenomewidemappingvivo2007} These findings contradicted earlier ideas of stringent binding to only canonical motifs, a rejection made possible only through the broadness of NGS. NGS also improved dynamic range compared to microarrays, showing for example that STAT1 motif enrichment was strongly correlated to absolute ChIP-seq peak height\sjcitep[.]{robertsonGenomewideProfilesSTAT12007}

% Gene-proximal (e.g. promoters) and distal (identified through comparative genomics or CpG enrichment) regulatory regions could be scanned for enrichment of known TF binding motifs sourced from databases such as TRANSFAC\sjcitep[,]{wingender2000transfac, matys2006transfac} or even parsed for de novo motif identification\sjcitep[.]{thijs2002gibbs} Significantly enriched motifs could in turn be used to reconstruct gene regulatory network candidates\sjcitep[.]{aerts2003toucan,aerts2005toucan} ready for reporter assay testing. \verb|endeavour|\sjcitep[]{aertsGenePrioritization2006} aggregated data from 8 independent databases including expression data\sjcitep[,]{barrett2005ncbi} GO terms\sjcitep[,]{ashburner2000gene} regulatory elements\sjcitep[,]{matys2006transfac} to rank gene associations with a training set (e.g. genes already known to be associated with a certain disease). Tools like these now allowed researchers to prioritized candidate genomic elements, such as novel genes, for further lab validation.

The increased genomic resolution of ChIP-seq was rapidly refining models of gene regulation, but the technique still required a strong antibody and prior knowledge of the protein or histone modification of interest. In contrast, DNase I hypersensitivity (DHS) assays could survey chromatin accessibility genome-wide. The technique had already been applied for genome-wide accessibility studies, first by Southern blot\sjcitep[,]{wu1980heat} then capillary Sanger sequencing\sjcitep[,]{crawford2004identifying} and later microarrays\sjcitep[.]{crawford2006dnase} Like ChIP, DNase-seq was soon translated to NGS\sjcitep[,]{boyle2008high,hesselberth2009global} rapidly contributing to even higher resolution (1 basepair) understanding of gene gene regulation. For example, matched DNase-seq and glucocorticoid receptor (GR) ChIP-seq revealed that out of \num{8236} active GR binding sites in murine mammary adenocarcinoma, 95\% were already accessible \textit{prior to} receptor activation\sjcitep[.]{john2011chromatin} These findings rejected that GR might act as a pioneer factor, remodeling inaccessible chromatin and exposing its preferred motif after receptor activation. Moreover, pituitary gland cells shared only 11.4\% of the binding sites accessible in adenocarcinoma, suggesting that differential chromatin context underlies differential responses to the same transcription factor. Unsurprisingly, ENCODE's second draft included DNAse-seq data from 125 tissues, along with H3K4me3 and TF ChIP-seq and bisulfite sequencing\sjcitep[.]{thurman2012accessible,encode2012} In total, 2.9 million DNase I hypersensitivity sites were identified and annotated genome-wide, covering almost all previously known regulatory element candidates: 94.4\% of previously known TF ChIP-seq regions were found to overlap with these DNase I sites, and only \num{3692} such sites were found in all cell types assessed, confirming that chromatin accessibility and downstream gene regulation are highly tissue and cell-type specific processes. Combination of these new regulatory readouts with GWAS further confirmed that many disease-related SNPs are located in regulatory regions, accelerating therapeutic target discovery.

By 2008, development of NGS began to displace microarrays in transcriptomics as well, first in yeast\sjcitep[,]{nagalakshmi2008transcriptional} and later in mouse\sjcitep[,]{mortazavi2008mapping} and human\sjcitep[.]{wang2008alternative} In the Mortazavi protocol, poly(A)\textsuperscript{+} mRNA was extracted and purified from brain, liver, and skeletal muscle from C57BL/6 mice, hydrolised and reverse-transcribed to cDNA using random hexamer priming. Illumina/Solexa was used to sequence the resulting libraries. This particular study delivered a groundbreaking number of new observations through the first quantitative whole transcriptome in mouse:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
	\item \textasciitilde{\num{145000}} distinct splicing events detected, with \textasciitilde{\num{3500}} genes showing alternative splicing.
	\item \textasciitilde{\num{3000}} newly identified or extended 5' exons,
	\item \textasciitilde{\num{3000}} novel extended 3' UTRs.
	\item \textasciitilde{\num{17000}} newly annotated transcribed regions
	\item \num{596} entirely novel candidate transcripts, including a previously unknown mir-124-1 precursor in the brain.
\end{itemize}

In addition, quantification of phage \textlambda{} RNA spike-ins remained linear (R\textsuperscript{2} = 0.99) over 5 orders of magnitude, and correlation between technical replicates was high (R\textsuperscript{2} = 0.96). A technical comparison of RNA-seq with microarray transcriptomics showed that RNA-seq was technically superior in every aspect:\sjcitep[]{marioni2008rna} RNA-seq identified 30\% more differentially expressed genes than microarrays at the same false discovery rate, and the sequencing approach was better at detecting low-abundance genes. qPCR validation also confirmed more RNA-seq-specific findings than microarray-specific findings. Finally, \textasciitilde{\num{200000}} RNA-seq reads mapped to possible exon-exon junctions, revealing novel splicing patterns. Each RNA extract was sequenced 7 times, unsurprisingly with a Spearman correlation of 0.96 across lanes, but extraction itself was not replicated\sjcitep[.]{wang2009rna} Several major findings emerged from RNA-seq. Eukaryotic transcriptomes turned out to be more heterogeneic than anticipated. Individual papers revealed large numbers of novel transcribed regions, widespread antisense transcription, and extensive variation in 3' ends\sjcitep[.]{wang2008alternative}

By 2010, next-generation sequencing costs itself had become widely available, but library prep still required sonication, end-repair, and adapter ligation. A breakthrough came from Tn5, an enzyme first studied decades earlier. Barbara McClintock had first observed "mutable loci" in maize arise from mobile DNA inserts\sjcitep[,]{mcclintock1950,ravindran2012} the first observation of position-effect variegation and cis-regulatory chromatin influence outside of \textit{Drosophila}\sjcitep[.]{muller1930} Two decades later, foreign DNA insertions were observed to disrupt the \textit{E. coli} \textit{gal} operon\sjcitep[.]{shapiro1969} Later, entire resistance plasmids were found to integrate into phage \textlambda{}, flanked by 1.5 kb inverted repeats\sjcitep[.]{berg1975} The transposable element was later named Tn5, a member of an entire class of transposons\sjcitep[.]{bukhari1977} By 1982, both inverted repeats, named IS50-L and IS50-R, were sequenced\sjcitep[.]{reznikoff1981,reznikoff1982} IS50-R encodes the 461 amino-acid primary transposase as well as a truncated version of the protein. This smaller protein is expressed from a second, downstream transcription start site and inhibits the complete transposase by competitive binding, repressing total transposition activity. A conserved stop codon point mutation found in the nearly identical IS50-L repeat disrupts complete expression of the transposase there, but simultaneously introduces a promotor sequence for the embedded antibiotic resistance genes. The Tn5 transposon is thus capable of transferring antibiotic resistance genes and expressing them independently of the host's endogenous mechanisms. It also self-regulates both its transposon and resistance activities. Soon, Tn5's potential as a molecular tool was recognised. For example, in-vitro Tn5 insertions could be used to disrupt genes in bacteria, with antibiotic resistance as a positive selection marker\sjcitep[.]{ruvkun1982} A first full crystallography of Tn5 protein (Tnp) in complex with DNA showed that it shares mechanistic features with HIV-1 integrase and RAG1/2 V(D)J recombinases\sjcitep[.]{davies2000threedimensional}

Owing to its use as a lab tool, Tn5 protein mutants and their molecular mechanisms were quickly studied\sjcitep[.]{wiegand1992characterization, weinreich1994evidence} A triple mutant, E54K (enhances OE binding) - M56A (disrupts inhibitor production) - L372P (increases activity by altering dimerization), was engineered to increase catalytic activity, and is the major variant in use to date ("hyperactive Tn5")\sjcitep[.]{goryshintn5vitrotransposition1998} The transposase can bind to two sites on the ends of each IS50 sequence: the inside or outside end (IE, OE). A synthetic "mosaic end" (ME), engineered by combining elements of the IE and the OE showed enhanced binding capacity, improving dimerization and complexation of Tnp with DNA\sjcitep[.]{zhou1998molecular,steinigerwhiteEvidenceUnseenTransposase2002} The transposase's exact catalytic mechanism was unknown until Bhasin and colleagues showed how the Tn5 transposase nicks at the transposon ends, forms a hairpin, releases the donor dna, and opens the hairpin and integrates at the acceptor site\sjcitep[.]{bhasin1999hairpin} A staggered cut is made at the acceptor site, and after insertion of the blunt-end transposon, the missing 9 base pairs are repaired, leading to a duplication. A classic DDE motif, known from RNAseH enzymes, in Tn5 transposase's active site coordinates magnesium ions critical for catalysis by stabilizing the DNA-protein complexs\sjcitep[.]{lovell2002twometal,reznikoffTn5ModelUnderstanding2003}

By the mid 2000s, Tn5 saw routine lab use to tag cloned DNA with selection markers, primers, or even regulatory elements\sjcitep[.]{reznikoffTn5MolecularGenetics2004} This could be achieved without in vivo expression of the transposon by loading transposase protein onto pre-cleaved DNA, and introducing the complex into electroporated bacteria. Tn5 transposase was first used by Adey and Shendure to construct shotgun sequencing libraries in 2010\sjcitep[.]{adeyRapidLowInputLowbias2010} Previously, researchers needed to fragment DNA using sonication or restriction enzymes, followed by end repair and ligation, a process that spanned 6 to 10 hours. With Tn5 transposase "tagmentation", these pre-PCR steps were reduced to only 15 to 20 minutes. Moreover, the Adey protocol could use inputs as low as \SI{10}{\pico\gram} as opposed to the \SI{1}{\micro\gram} required by the ligation methods, a sensitivity increase that would later allow library extraction from single-cells.

Buenrostro and colleagues realized around 2013 that a hyper-active Tn5 dimer pre-loaded with Illumina adapters could be used not only to construct sequencing libraries, but also to interrogate the accessibility of native chromatin\sjcitep[.]{buenrostro2013transposition,buenrostro2015atacseq} Transposition was performed on intact, unfixed nuclei, with Tn5 dimers sterically hindered by occupant nucleosomes and other DNA-bound proteins. Every insertion event marks an open stretch of chromatin, and, since Tn5 already ligates sequencing adapters, subsequent PCR generates a sequencing-ready library. The resulting protocol, named ATAC-seq (assay for transposase-accessible chromatin by sequencing), required just \num{50000} cells - compared to the 1 to 100 million cells needed for DNAse-seq, FAIRE-seq and ChIP-seq. Omni-ATAC, an improved protocol, introduced the triple-detergent lysis buffer (NP-40 + Tween-20 + digitonin), and optimized magnesium concentrations, dramatically reducing mitochondrial contamination and extending the assay to frozen tissue sections\sjcitep[.]{corces2017improved} These optimisations resulted in a 13-fold drop in mitochondrial reads and 3-fold rise in fraction of reads in peaks, and up to 15-fold increased number of unique fragments at similar sequencing depths.

\section{Single-cell RNA and ATAC-seq protocols}
\label{sect:single_cell_sequencing_tech}
The development of next-generation sequencing methods and quantitative -omics assays in the early 2000s - most notably RNA-seq, ChIP-seq, DNase-seq, and later ATAC-seq - allowed researchers to probe gene expression and regulatory elements in high-throughput, at genome-wide scales (\autoref{fig:sequencing_assays}). Application of these technologies revealed tissue- and cell type-specific enhancer landscapes, complex transcription factor networks, novel transcript isoforms and splicing patterns, and altered chromatin states across disease and development. Together, these bulk methods laid conceptual frameworks for understanding the genome, and significantly advanced our understanding of development, immunity, and cancer.

However, bulk assays cannot distinguish signals from different populations within the input sample. Unexpected heterogeneity, rare cell states, and stochastic dynamics are masked by the aggregation of thousands to millions of cells. The combination of FAC sorting and quantitative bulk sequencing methods can help, but is costly, laborious, and requires prior knowledge. Even in combination with cell-sorting, bulk assays remained unsuitable for an umber of key open problems:\sjcitep[]{shapiro2013single}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
	\item Profiling of rare or transient cell-types, such as circulating tumor cells and metastatic cancer subclones.
	\item Profiling the full complexity of systems composed of potentially thousands of cell types, such as brains and the repertoire of immune cells.
	\item Reconstructing cell lineages across development, differentiation and disease progression in high temporal and potentially spatial resolution.
	\item Systematically defining cell types and identifying consensus markers, both in expression, but also in active genomic elements and modifications.
	\item Quantifying stochastic differences between cells sampled from the same type.
	\item High-throughput genetic or chemical perturbation studies.
\end{itemize}

Some of these considerations drove early exploration of gene expression at the single-cell level in the 90s, starting a decade-long quest for methods to amplify RNA material from individual cells. Eberwine and colleagues first injected an oligo-dT T7 primer and T7 reverse transcriptase directly into single rat hippocampal neurons to reverse transcribe their mRNA in situ\sjcitep[.]{eberwine1992analysis} The resulting cDNA was amplified using in vitro transcription, and relative expression levels of 10 known neuronal genes (mostly ion channels) were quantified by Southern blot. Even among \textit{morphologically similar neurons}, significant differences in gene expression were observed, recording for the first time transcriptomic heterogeneity within the same tissue. About a decade later, a double T7-based amplification method was applied to profile 12 laser microdissected rat hippocampal neurons on cDNA microarrays, revealing two new marker genes for a novel pyramidal neuron subtype\sjcitep[.]{kamme2003singlecell} Soon, PCR-based cDNA amplification strategies\sjcitep[]{brady1993construction,brady2000expression} rather than IVT was applied to single cells from mouse embryonic stem cells and blastomeres\sjcitep[.]{kurimoto2006improved} Mature mRNA was extracted by poly(dT) primers, reverse-transcribed and PCR-amplified, and the resulting single-cell libraries were each hybridised to \textit{individual} RNA microarrays. This revealed that the blastocyst's inner cell mass contains at least two populations: some express genes typical of primitive endoderm (\textit{Gata4}, \textit{Gata6}) and others express epiblast markers (\textit{Fgf4}, \textit{Nanog}, \textit{Klf2}).

%Radiolabeled aRNAs were also hybridised to a cDNA library extracted from NG108, a hybrid neuroblastoma-glioma cell line known to express a wide variety of neuron-associated genes. Hybridising clones were isolated and sequenced, leading to the discovery of three novel genes that did not match existing gene databases.

"True" whole transcriptome single-cell sequencing was first demonstrated in 2009 by Tang and Surani at the Gurdon Institute\sjcitep[.]{tangMRNASeqWholeTranscriptome2009} By increasing reverse transcription and PCR extension times/cycles, the group was able to amplify enough material for sequencing cDNA libraries from 6 individual mouse blastomeres. This sequencing-based approach provided a number of immediate advantages over microarray analysis:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
	\item Comparison of single-blastomere mRNA-Seq data with bulk (320 pooled cells) Affymetrix microarray data showed that \SI{94.3}{\percent} of microarray-detected genes had at least 5 transcripts in the mRNA-Seq data, but mRNA-Seq detected \num{5270} (up to 75\%) more genes in total.
	\item Sensitivity and dynamic range (\SIrange{0.2}{12000}{RPKM}) were decidedly higher for mRNA-Seq than for analog microarrays.
	\item Since near-whole transcripts were sequenced novel splice junctions could be determined. The group discovered \num{1753} novel isoforms, demonstrated for the first time that different isoforms of the same gene can be expressed in the same single cell.
\end{enumerate}

Tang et al. used the ABI SOLiD sequencer, which had become commercially available only two years prior. Since the sample extraction and amplification protocol itself were only slightly different from existing microarray protocols, it can be posed that development of the Tang method was an effect of sequencing hardware improvements. However, the new sequencing-based approach also came with a new set of challenges. Cost of the novel sequencing technology was very high - each cell in the Tang paper, sequenced at around 24 to 85 million reads, must have cost around \USD{10000} to \USD{50000} in SOLiD sequencing alone \textit{per cell}. At the time, one Affymetric microarray sample cost in the order of \USD{800} - significantly cheaper. Despite longer extension times, the protocol was also unable to capture transcripts longer than \SI{3}{kb}, and was limited to poly(A)\textsuperscript{+} mRNA. These factors contributed to mRNA-Seq's status as a proof-of-concept technique rather than an immediate research tool.

One of the first protocols to be published after the Tang protocol was STRT-seq (single-cell tagged reverse transcription), developed by the Linnarson lab at Karolinska Institute\sjcitep[.]{islamCharacterizationSinglecellTranscriptional2011, islamHighlyMultiplexedStrandSpecific2012} The protocol applied template-switching reverse transcription (use of MMLV reverse transcriptase, which tags 5' transcripts via template switching)\sjcitep[]{zhu2001reverse} to cells picked into individual wells, followed by pooled single-primer PCR for cDNA amplification. A total of 92 mouse cells were profiled: 48 embryonic stem cells and 44 embryonic feeder cells. Libraries were sequenced on the Illumina Genome Analyzer IIx, for a total of 110M raw reads. mRNA capture efficiency was approximately 10\%, as measured by RNA spike-in panels (ERCC). 80\% of filtered reads aligned to exonic and splice-junction features in the mouse genome, which is high even by today's standards. Introduction of degenerate bases (\verb|NNNNNNNNNN|) in the 5' adapter allowed multiple counts of PCR duplicates to be collapsed into transcript counts\sjcitep[.]{kiviojaCountingAbsoluteNumbers2011} Addition of these unique molecular adapters (UMIs) to the STRT-seq protocol, plus parallel automated cell capture on microfluidic cell capture arrays (Fluidigm C1), and oligo design optimisation improved the capture efficiency of STRT-seq from \textasciitilde{10}\% to \textasciitilde{50}\% \sjcitep[.]{islamQuantitativeSingleCell2014}

% Later on, STRT-seq was also applied to custom-milled 9600-well nano arrays, recovering between 3000 to 7500 single cells per plate and an average of \num{8706} UMIs and \num{3686} genes\sjcitep[.]{hochgerner2017strt}

% Tumor heterogeneity was already known to underlie treatment resistance\sjcitep[.]{park2010cellular} Bulk DNA sequencing and comparative genomic hybridization assays cannot identify heterogenity in mutation or copy number variation profile. Navin et al. FAC sorted 200 single cells from triple-negative breast cancer tumors, and performed whole genome amplification and sequencing on individual cells\sjcitep[.]{navin2011tumour} Due to low per-cell coverage (\textasciitilde{6\%}), copy number variations was the focus of this study. Lineage analysis identified several "bursts" of CNV shared across main subpopulations, challenging previous ideas of slow, gradual accumulation of mutations underlying cancer. Metastases were strongly enriched for aneuploid populations that represent minorities in the primary tumor, indicating that chromosomal duplication helps establish rapidly expanding metastatic clones. "By isolating, sorting and sequencing the genome of 52 cells from a primary breast tumour and 48 cells from its associated liver metastasis, Navin et al. showed that a single cell from the primary tumor mass seeded the metastatic tumour\sjcitep[.]{navinTumourEvolutionInferred2011}"

In parallel research, also at the Karolinska Institute, Ramsk\"{o}ld and Sandberg developed Smart-seq (switching mechanism at the 5' end of RNA template)\sjcitep[.]{ramskoldFulllengthMRNASeqSinglecell2012} Similarly to STRT-seq, a combination of MMLV reverse-transcriptase followed by cDNA PCR was used. However, fragmentation of these full-length transcripts using Tn5 (or Covaris shearing) allowed for shotgun sequencing of the entire transcript. 42 cells from several human cancer cell lines, circulating tumor cells, and mouse oocytes were profiled. Improved isoform coverage showed that exon 13 of the NEDD4L gene, a prognosis marker, was differentially expressed between LNCaP prostate cancer and T24 bladder cancer cells. A year later, an updated Smart-seq protocol with increased sensitivity was published\sjcitep[.]{picellismartseq2sensitivefull2013} Here, the addition of a locked nucleic acid (LNA) at the TSO's 3' end and addition of betaine led to 2-4 fold cDNA yield increases. The workflow was also simplified and reduced in cost by switching to KAPA HiFi polymerase, which reduced GC-biases. The use of in-house produced recombinant Tn5\sjcitep[]{picellitn5transposasetagmentation2014} further reduced costs for Smart-seq2\sjcitep[]{picellifulllengthrnaseqsingle2014} - researchers could now bypass Illumina's Nextera Tn5, which to this day is still enormously expensive (\textasciitilde\$\num{3600} per \SI{160}{\micro\liter}). A quantitative comparison of Smart-seq and bulk RNA-seq revealed that aggregate Smart-seq data closely resembles bulk RNA-seq data from 1 million cells, and correlates strongly (Pearson R = 0.9) to ERCC spike-ins\sjcitep[.]{wuquantitativeassessmentsinglecell2014} Moreover, nanoliter volume reactions used in the Fluidigm C1 consistently identified more genes and had more uniform transcript coverage than microliter volume reactions due to higher enzyme, template and substrate concentrations and lower loss in washes. Continued development led to further sensitivity improvements in Smart-seq3\sjcitep[.]{hagemannjensensinglecellrnacounting2020} CEL-seq, developed in 2012, elected to use linear amplification by in-vitro transcription after reverse transcription rather than PCR-based approaches - effectively translating the 1992 Eberwine protocol to sequencing\sjcitep[.]{hashimshonycelseqsinglecellrnaseq2012} The protocol generated \num{280000} mRNA counts from single \textit{C. elegans} blastomeres. An updated protocol later increased low capture efficiency from \textasciitilde{6}\% to \textasciitilde{22}\% and increased throughput to 96-format using the now trending Fluidigm C1\sjcitep[.]{hashimshony2016celseq2}

In 2013, Sasagawa et al. argued that Smart-seq, CEL-seq and STRT-seq suffered from amplification biases caused by 3' enrichment, template switching, and pseudo-random Tn5 fragmentation, resulting in poor correlation between technical replicates (Pearson R between 0.7 and 0.85)\sjcitep[.]{sasagawa2013quartz} Their method, Quartz-seq, showed several sensitivity and specificity improvements: by employing Exo I digestion after reverse transcription and cDNA Covaris instead of Tn5 fragmentation, the amount of adapter sequences present in the data was reduced from 21\% to 7\%, and technical reproducibility was increased (Pearson R of \textasciitilde{0.93}). Later, Quartz-seq2 would improve throughput by using FAC sorting into 1536-wells, and reduce costs from \$23 to \$0.50 per cell by earlier pooling of the reactions\sjcitep[.]{sasagawa2018quartzseq2} Stringent live-cell selection with FACS, addition of a UMI to the RT primer, optimized poly(dA) tailing of cDNA, and use of Y-shaped Illumina adapters further reduced biases and increased sensitivity, achieving a "UMI conversion rate" of 30-50\%, compared to Quartz-seq1 and Drop-seq's 10\%, and CEL-seq's 22\%\sjcitep[.]{ziegenhainComparativeAnalysis2017, mereu2020benchmarking}

At this point in time, the first plate-based scRNA-seq protocols - particularly Smart-seq - began to spread to other labs, and single-cell sequencing was elected Nature method of the year in 2013\sjcitep[.]{MethodYear20132014} For example, analysis of \num{430} single glioblastoma cells with Smart-seq showed heterogeneous expression of several receptor tyrosine kinases (EGFR, PDGFRA, FGFR1), potentially leading to redundancy and therapeutic resistance\sjcitep[.]{patelSingleCellRNAseq2014} The existence of CD133+, therapy-resistant "glioblastoma stem-like cells" had been shown before\sjcitep[,]{baogliomastemcells2006} but single-cell technologies made deeper characterisation possible. However, due to the high reagent and labour costs per cell (approximately \USD30 per cell plus cost of sequencing), almost all studies from this period were limited to few (hundreds) of cells, mainly sourced from few-cell stage embryonic samples\sjcitep[.]{kolodziejczyktechnologybiologysinglecell2015} Profiling samples with inherently large cell counts and diversity, such as immune systems and neuron clusters, would require a different approach.

One strategy to increase cell throughput of pure plate-based methods is to decrease costs by pooling reactions early. In MARS-seq\sjcitep[,]{jaitinMassivelyParallelSingleCell2014} cells are FAC sorted into 384 well-plates, where they undergo cell-barcoded reverse transcription. Reactions are then pooled, and second-strand synthesis and further reactions occur in a shared microtube. cDNA is then amplified by IVT, fragmented, reverse transcribed, amplified via PCR, and sequenced. Applying this to \textasciitilde{\num{5500}} mouse spleen cells led to the discovery of novel dendritic cell subpopulations, exhibiting activation of different \textit{Irf4}, \textit{TNF}, and \textit{TGF-\textbeta} pathways in response to LPS stimulation.

Several scaling breakthroughs were made in 2015, the "annus mirabilis" for single-cell sequencing methods. First, droplet microfluidics\sjcitep[]{thorsen2001dynamic} was applied to single-cell sequencing. Researchers at David Weitz' lab had been working on using nanoliter droplets to perform antibody screening\sjcitep[]{mazutis2013singlecell} and rare template PCR, referring to a hypothetical system of encapsulating molecules with a nucleic acid barcode that \textit{"indicates which element is encapsulated in the droplet"}\sjcitep[.]{guoDropletMicrofluidicsHighthroughput2012} This system was realised for single-cell RNA-seq in 2015 by two separate labs, both at Harvard in collaboration with Weitz lab: Macosko and colleagues (McCarroll lab) developed Drop-seq\sjcitep[,]{macoskohighlyparallelgenomewide2015} and Klein and colleagues (Kirschner lab) developed inDrop\sjcitep[.]{kleinDropletBarcodingSinglecell2015,zilionissinglecellbarcodingsequencing2017}

Both methods used droplet microfluidics to encapsulate single-cells in nanoliter volume droplets together with a bead carrying barcoded poly(dT) primers, but their amplification and library preparation strategies differed. Drop-seq used a Smart-seq-like approach: once encapsulated, cells are lysed, and their polyA\textsuperscript{+} mRNA is captured by the beads, which carry a poly(dT) oligonucleotide primer containing a UMI and a cell barcode. The emulsion is then broken, and beads are pooled and washed in saline sodium citrate to minimize cross-bead hybridizations. The beads, still with mRNA hybridized, are resuspended in a reverse transcriptase mix, and RT proceeds on-bead to produce full-length cDNA. After RT, exonuclease I is applied to degrade any unextended primers, reducing noise from unused barcode sequences. The resulting cDNA, which is covalently linked to the bead, is then PCR-amplified. Fragmentation proceeds using Nextera XT Tn5 to generate a sequencing library. All steps involving enzymes happen in a pooled manner, saving reagent cost. Drop-seq's cell purity (absence of cell doublets) was assessed using a mixture of HEK (human) and 3T3 (mouse) cell lines, and ranged between 90.4\% to 98.8\% based on cell concentration used. The method was demonstrated on \num{44808} cells from the P14 mouse retina generated in 7 runs across 4 days, producing 39 cell clusters. \textit{PPP1R17}, a new marker for the poorly-characterized nGnG (non-glycinergic/non-GABAergic) amacrine cells, was identified and validated by histochemistry. Transcript capture efficiency was estimated at \textasciitilde10.7 to 12.8\% using ddPCR validation. Drop-seq marked the first single-cell dataset spanning tens of thousands of cells, and reported a >100-fold reduction in cost and time compared to prior scRNA-seq methods (\textasciitilde6.5 cents per cell, \num{10000} cells in 12 hours). Later, a microfluidics-free version of the protocol, using bead arrays rather than droplets, was developed\sjcitep[.]{gierahnseqwellportablelowcost2017}

inDrop used a different approach to both cell loading and reaction chemistry: cells are encapsulated with soft, deformable hydrogels rather than solid resin beads like Drop-seq, which can be stacked in the microfluidic channel. This ordered stack of beads allows almost every droplet to receive a bead, rather than \textasciitilde{2}\% for the poissonian loading used in Drop-seq\sjcitep[.]{abate2009beating} Reaction chemistry was also different. After encapsulation, UV light releases the barcoded poly(dT) primers from the bead into the droplet, and reverse transcription happens within the droplet. Next, the CEL-seq IVT strategy is used to amplify the barcoded, single-cell transcriptomes in a pooled manner. The protocol was demonstrated on a total of \num{11209} mouse embryonic stem cells, including a time series of leukemia inhibitory factor (LIF) withdrawal-induced differentiation over 7 days. 5 distinct ES sub-populations were identified, including rare lineages marked by epiblast (\textit{Krt8/18}), primitive endoderm (\textit{Col4a1/2}, \textit{Lama1}), and heat shock markers (\textit{Hsp90}, \textit{Hspa5}). Counts agreed strongly with smFISH data (R = 0.92 for mean, 0.90 for CV).

Both Drop-seq and inDrop boasted a significant jump in throughput over pure plate-based sorting methods, from hundreds to several thousands, potentially tens of thousands, of cells per day. Higher cell counts, traded for per-cell sensitivity, now allowed for the profiling of highly varied samples such as immune cells. However, both methods also required fabrication of complex reagents (notably, barcoded beads and microfluidic chips) and complex microfluidic operation. In 2016, the first commercial droplet-microfluidic single-cell sequencing solution was released: 10x Genomics' 3' Single Cell Gene Expession kit and accompanying Chromium microfluidic controller combined barcoded hydrogel beads, enzymes, primers and microfluidics in a relatively simple package. The method drew on concepts from both inDrop and Drop-seq, opting for deformable hydrogel beads for higher cell capture, and a SMART-seq-like, PCR + Tn5 amplification and library preparation strategy. Importantly, the Chromium could load up to 8 samples in parallel, simplifying arrayed experiments. The method was first demonstrated on \num{68000} PBMCs sampled from 8 donors, identifying all major PBMC subtypes (T, NK, B, myeloid) and minor populations (e.g. megakaryocytes)\sjcitep[.]{zhengmassivelyparalleldigital2017} Approximately \num{4500} genes and \num{27000} UMIs were detected on average per cell at a depth of 100k reads/cell, an order of magnitude higher than inDrop and Drop-seq. Mixed-species loading revealied a doublet rate of approximately 3\%.

2015's second leap came when researchers in Shendure lab applied combinatorial indexing to single cells in-situ\sjcitep[,]{cusanovich2015multiplex} also marking the first single-cell ATAC sequencing protocol (sci-ATAC-seq, single-cell combinatorial indexing ATAC sequencing). In sci-ATAC, nuclei are tagmented using Tn5 loaded with an array of barcoded adapters in 96-well plates and re-distributed into a second 96-well plate containing PCR mixes with 96 different primer combinations. Each nucleus is thus indexed by a combination of its Tn5 and PCR barcode, and individual cells are never physically separated. The method was applied to \num{14533} cells from 4 different cell lines. Between 50 and 60\% of reads were located in ENCODE-defined DNaseI hypersensitivity sites, confirming assay fidelity. Approximately \num{5000} accessible fragments are captured per cell on average. Cross-cell collision rate was estimated at 11\% using species identities in the cell lines, and individual cell barcodes contained \textasciitilde90\% species-pure accessible fragments. Interestingly, 4 different states were observed in GM12878 cells based on accessible enhancers. The same combinatorial indexing strategy was applied to scRNA-seq in 2017, this time profiling \num{49360} \textit{C. elegans} L2 larvae, approximately 50-fold coverage over its 762 somatic cells at this stage\sjcitep[.]{caoComprehensiveSinglecellTranscriptional2017} Instead of $96 \times 96$, split-pooling was performed over $576 \times 960$ possible wells (first during in-situ reverse transcription, then Tn5 fragmentation and PCR). Median UMIs and genes detected in \textit{C. elegans} was 575 UMIs and 431 respectively, identifying 27 major cell types, including rare neurons with only 1 or 2 cells per larva. Reagent costs were estimated at \USD0.03-\USD0.20 per cell. Updated protocols were published in 2022, offering higher sensitivity and lower costs (\USD0.01 per cell)\sjcitep[.]{martinOptimizedSingleNucleus2023} SPLiT-seq (split-pool ligation-based transcriptome sequencing)\sjcitep[,]{rosenberg2018singlecell} an independently developed combinatorial indexing protocol, introduced additional barcode ligation steps to increase barcode complexity to 96\textsuperscript{4}. The method was first applied to generate \num{156049} single-cell transcriptomes from P2 and P11 mouse brains and spinal cords, and was recently commercialized into kits by Parse Biosciences\sjcitep[]{kuijpers2024split} - offering up to 10 million cells indexed per run\sjcitep[.]{parse2025gigalab}

Buenrostro and Greenleaf also released a protocol for ATAC-seq at single-cell level in 2015\sjcitep[.]{buenrostrosinglecellchromatinaccessibility2015} Cells are loaded onto 96-plex chips on the Fluidigm C1, and lysed in the nanoliter reaction chamber. Nuclei are then incubated with Tn5 and washed with an EDTA or SDS buffer, releasing Tn5 in-situ. Individual nuclei are PCR-cycled on-chip with barcoded primers, indexing cell identity. Libraries are then collected for further amplification, and sequenced. The C1 scATAC-seq protocol was applied to a consortium of GM12878 (human lymphoblastoid), K562 (human leukemia), H1 hESCs, V6.5 mouse ESCs, EML1 (mouse early hematopoietic), TF1 (human erythroblast), HL-60 (human promyeloblast), and BJ (fibroblast) for a total of \num{1632} cells profiled. On average, \num{73000} fragments aligned to the genome, and around 35\% of reads were located in called peak regions. While the aggregate data was approximately 500-fold more sensitive than bulk ATAC-seq (normalized to cell counts), individual aggregate-accessible peaks contained an average of 0.10 fragments per cell - showing extreme sparsity. Pearson correlation between samples and correlation with ENCODE DNase-seq data was high (R = 0.86 and 0.80).

The sci-ATAC-seq and the Fluidigm C1 protocol require either a custom barcoded Tn5 (not widely available at the time), or an expensive, specialized microfluidics controller. Moreover, the C1 protocol lacked scale, and the sci protocol was low in sensitivity. A relatively simple and robust protocol, leaning on FAC sorting of bulk pre-tagmented nuclei into individual 96/384-well plates, and amplifying their accessible chromatin individually, was published in 2018, providing intermediate scale and sensitivity\sjcitep[.]{chenrapidrobustmethod2018} In 2019, two commercial solutions, both using high-throughput droplet-microfluidics, were released by BioRad and then 10x Genomics. BioRad's product, the ddSEQ SureCell ATAC, was a hard, resin bead-based protocol developed in conjunction with Buenrostro lab\sjcitep[.]{lareaudropletbasedcombinatorialindexing2019} Bulk pre-tagmented nuclei are encapsulated with barcoded hard resin beads on the ddSEQ microfluidic platform. Since super-poissonian loading cannot be achieved without hydrogel beads, only a small percentage of droplets would contain a bead under normal circumstances, resulting in a low cell capture rate of around 2\% - similar to Drop-seq. However, smart application of Tn5 tracking allowed the low capture rate to be circumvented: beads are "overloaded", leading to 2.5 beads rather than < 0.05 encapsulated per droplet on average. Each nucleus' material is thus split over multiple single-cell barcodes. Cell barcodes that are statistically significantly enriched for overlap between their captured fragments can be detected and merged in downstream analysis. The resulting cell capture rate for dscATAC-seq is reported as 95\% (\num{5324} cells recovered from \num{5600} loaded). Genomic alignment and sensitivity were also high, reporting 95\% alignment to reference, and a median of \num{34046} unique reads per cell with \num{58.8}\% in peaks. In addition, barcoded Tn5 ("dsci-ATAC") was demonstrated to further increase throughput to up to \num{100000} cells per device, for a total of \num{510123} single cells profiled in the study. In contrast to the BioRad product, the 10x Genomics solution used hydrogel beads, just like their gene expression kit\sjcitep[.]{satpathyMassivelyParallelSinglecell2019} Bulk pre-tagmented are encapsulated in droplets, where the bead dissolves and releases barcoded primers. In-situ PCR indexes single-nucleus accessible fragments, and a second PCR generates a sequencing-ready library. The method was applied to \num{61806} cells from blood and bone marrow across 16 donors and \num{37818} basal cell carcinoma cells from 7 patients, confirming tumor-specific variation in accessibility and copy numbers. An average of \num{27800} unique fragments were detected per cell, and between 31.5\ and 62.5\% of reads were located within peaks. Based on both articles, it thus appeared that the BioRad's dscATAC was more sensitive than the 10x solution.

A known problem with all classic ATAC-seq methods is that only fragments tagmented with an i5 adapter on one end an an i7 adapter on the other are PCR-amplified. Since adapter loading on the Tn5 dimer is a random process, and each insertion site is equally likely to receive either possible combination of i5 and i7 insertions, 50\% of all fragments generated are either i5-i5 or i7-i7 species. Symmetrical strand sci-ATAC (s3-ATAC) solves this problem by introducing a uracil base after Tn5's mosaic end adapters\sjcitep[.]{mulqueenhighcontentsinglecellcombinatorial2021} Uracil-intolerant polymerase is then used in the gap fill step step, generating fragments with incomplete overhang. These overhangs can be reliably targeted with a universal i5 adapter, resulting in fragments which are always i5- and i7-ended - effectively doubling capture.
Application on \num{2175} human and \num{837} mouse brain cells yielded between \num{100280} and \num{178069} median unique reads per cell, approximately 6-fold higher than 10x Genomics scATAC-seq, with between 48.1- and 78.2\% of reads falling in peaks.

\section{Scaling cell throughput}
The release of the 10x Chromium scRNA-seq kit marked a milestone in single-cell sequencing research. It was user friendly enough, and struck a good balance between cell throughput and single-cell quality. Several atlasing projects were completed using the technology at the hundreds of thousands of cell scale, including human\sjcitep[,]{regev2017human} mouse\sjcitep[,]{tabulamuris2018} and fly\sjcitep[.]{davie2018single} In the latter, \num{157000} high-quality single-cell \textit{D. melanogaster} transcriptomes were generated in 26 runs sampled across 8 timepoints between 0 and 50 days old. CEL-seq2 and SMART-seq2 data supplemented the medium-depth 10x Chromium cells with high-quality "anchors". Novel cell-type-specific gene regulatory networks were inferred from for olfactory projection neurons, and glia using SCENIC. Participant TFs were validated for motif enrichment in these cell types using FACS-assisted ATAC-seq.

Meanwhile, plate-based combinatorial indexing strategies like SPLiT-seq and the sci-family of protocols have in general skirted further the extreme end of the sensitivity/throughput scale, leading to their use in several million-cell scale atlasing projects (\autoref{table:millions}).

Scaling droplet-based methods to million cell numbers is also possible. Cells or nuclei can be distributed over 96-well plates and pre-indexed by reverse transcription with a barcoded primer akin to plate-based combinatorial indexing protocols ("scifi-RNA-seq")\sjcitep[.]{datlingerultrahighthroughput2021} The pre-indexed cells are then pooled and run through 10x Chromium droplet generation together at extremely overloaded concentrations: whereas the 10x Chromium was designed for \textasciitilde\num{10000} cells per channel, scifi-RNA-seq increases this to up to 1.53 million cells per channel without apparent reduction in per-cell data quality. The same principle can be applied to ATAC-seq: scifi-ATAC-seq and txci-ATAC-seq use pre-indexing in 96 wells with barcoded adapter Tn5, enabling the profiling of up to \num{200000} single nuclei per 10x scATAC-seq lane\sjcitep[.]{zhang2024scifi, zhang2024txci} Sensitivity also remained high, sampling between \num{21362} and \num{45622} unique fragments per cell on average across tissues assessed. Interestingly, these findings suggest that the chemical and physical reactions within the nanoliter droplots are not rate-limited by concentration of enzymes and reagents.

Similar approaches have been used not to increase pure throughput, but to scale number of samples per droplet run. Again, the main strategy here is to pre-index or "hash" samples with oligonucleotide barcodes. Hashed samples can then be co-processed through droplet generation together, and the sample hash can computationally separate cells into sample bins post hoc. Hashing can be done by attaching sample-barcoded oligonucleotides to their membranes, either by antibody-antigen binding\sjcitep[]{stoeckiusCellHashing2018} or lipid/cholesterol embedding\sjcitep[.]{mcginnisMultiSeqSample2019} Within each droplet, the membrane-bound oligonucleotides co-captured together with the associated cell's mRNA or tagmented genome. Antibodies have demonstrated slightly better accuracy, while lipid hashing can be used when high-strength antibodies are not available\sjcitep[.]{mylkacomparativeanalysisantibody2022} The increased precision of antibody-based methods could be attributed to the many successive washes involved, but which may also reduce cell viability and integrity.

Instead of surface antibodies, oligonucleotides can also directly index generic (not custom-barcoded) Tn5. SNuBar (single-nucleotide barcoding) introduces a single oligonucleotide barcode (SBO) during tagmentation\sjcitep[.]{wang2021simple} This SBO contains an 18 bp sample barcode and hybridises to Tn5 transposomes through a universal adapter sequence. During tagmentation these Tn5-SBO complexes remain stable as Tn5 integrates itself into accessible regions. Nuclei are then washed, removing unbound Tn5 and excess SBOs, and samples are pooled. After droplet encapsulation, the SBO is released from Tn5 and captured by the cell barcode during PCR amplification, linking cell barcodes with sample barcodes.

\section{Perturbations}
Large screens that investigate the effect of silencing specific genes using RNA interference emerged in the early 2000s\sjcitep[.]{berns2004large,boutros2006analysis} Such perturbation screens follow either an arrayed design, where perturbations are individually plated and tracked in physical space, or a pooled design, where cell lines are transduced with pooled shRNA vector libraries at a given MOI, and survivors are sequenced. When CRISPR was developed as a molecular tool for rapid and accurate gene editing in 2013\sjcitep[,]{ran2013genome} full genetic knockouts became widely available, producing a pooled genome-wide screen involving \num{64000} sgRNAs for \num{18000} gene knockouts just a year later\sjcitep[.]{shalem2014genome, behan2019prioritization, pacini2021integrated} In addition to genetic knockouts, specific genes can also be up- or downregulated using catalytically dead Cas9 fused to transcription activators such as VP64 or p65, or repressors such as KRAB. Both arrayed and pooled CRISPR/RNAi screens have drawbacks: arrayed screens require vast amounts of labour and reagents, and pooled CRISPR-screens are limited to very simple readouts such as cell survival or marker expression.

In 2016, researchers at the Broad institute devised that CRISPR edits could be performed in a pooled manner with a read-out at the single-cell level - leveraging the newly developed scRNA-seq technologies to also read perturbation identity at the single-cell level\sjcitep[.]{dixit2016perturbseq} In this method, Perturb-seq, each vector is designed to express two transcripts after integrating in the host genome: a first transcript containing the sgRNA, driven by U6, and a second transcript containing a puromycin selection cassette, BFP, and a 20 nucleotide long guide barcode (GBC), driven by EF1\textalpha{}. The GBC transcript is polyadenylated, allowing it to be captured and counted through standard 3' scRNA-seq protocols (10x GEX in this specific case). The \{sgRNA:GBC\} dictionary can then be used to identify the genetic edit present in each captured cell\sjcitep[.]{dixit2016perturbseq} This pooled approach allows rapid parallelisation of perturbation screens with limited labour. Using this system, researchers observed that even within the same perturbation, individual cells can exhibit different responses. In particular, knockdown of the key ER chaperone BiP (\textit{HSPA5}) caused two possible responses: some cells preferentially activated \textit{PERK}, while others activated \textit{IRE1\textalpha{}}. TAP-seq (Targeted Perturb-seq) improves on the original protocol by using targeted gene panel amplification rather than whole-transcriptome analysis\sjcitep[.]{schraivogel2020targeted} 96\% of sequenced reads mapped to target genes compared to \textasciitilde10\% in typical Perturb-seq experiments, achieving the same analytical power as Perturb-seq at 19- to 49-fold lower cost. Application to a dCas9-KRAB vector with sgRNAs targeting \num{1788} candidate enhancers at MOI of 1 identified 81 new enhancer-target gene pairs. These 81 enhancers affected 24\% of the genes in the amplification panel, a hit rate far higher than previous Perturb-seq screens (3.5 - 6.0\%). The assay's costs can be further scaled down by multiplexing perturbations or cells: if gene regulatory networks are assumed sparse in number of participants, then the differential expression of a cell responding to multiple perturbations (high MOI), or a droplet containing multiple single-perturbation cells, can be considered a linear combination of the effect of its components. Pooling cells or perturbations in this manner and demultiplexing the individual terms using PCA can reduce cost ten-fold and quantify interaction terms between different perturbations\sjcitep[.]{yao2024scalable} CROP-seq, a different perturbation platform, uses a poly-adenylated gRNA which is directly captured by 3' scRNA-seq rather than guide barcodes expressed on the selection cassette (\autoref{fig:perturbation_vectors})\sjcitep[.]{datlinger2017pooled} The method was demonstrated using Drop-seq instead of 10x Chromium, showing that the open-source method has sufficient sensitivity to detect the gRNA transcripts. A small screen of Jurkat T cells, knocking out 6 genes and 23 TFs involved in T-cell receptor signaling pathways (87 gRNAs total), confirmed that \textit{LCK}, \textit{ZAP70}, and \textit{LAT} knockouts blocked TCR induction. A total of 5,798 high-quality single-cell transcriptomes were analyzed, with a median of 80 cells per targeted gene.

Perturbation screens have also been coupled with scATAC-seq readouts. In Perturb-ATAC\sjcitep[,]{rubin2019coupled} cells are transduced in pools with Perturb-seq-like vectors. Single cells were then captured and tagmented on the Fluidigm C1, limiting the size of the study to \textasciitilde\num{4300} cells total. The GBCs are reverse-transcribed on-chip, allowing them to be amplified and captured through the ensuing ATAC PCR to generate perturbation-coupled scATAC-seq profiles. Dual knockdown of \textit{EBF1} and \textit{EZH2} (in B lympholbasts), and \textit{KLF4} and \textit{ZNF750} (in keratinocytes), showed stronger repression than individual perturbations, and this relationship was observed to be cell-type specific. Spear-ATAC\sjcitep[]{pierce2021high} is able to overcome Perturb-ATAC's throughput limitations by using droplet encapsulation on 10x Chromium. After pooled lentiviral transduction, cells undergo the regular 10x scATAC-seq workflow, but with an additional primer added to the droplet encapsulation step. This primer anneals to the integrated lentiviral cassette, amplifying its barcode. By using droplet-based methods, \num{414} sgRNA knockdowns could be profiled across \textasciitilde\num{105000} cells (sampled between 3 and 21 days post transduction). Analyzing the resulting time series revealed time-dependent effects of the knockdowns. For example, \textit{GATA1} knockdown was found to first upregulate \textit{STAT5} motif accessibility, but later \textit{RUNX} motifs. As expected, these GRNs were also observed to be cell-type specific between K562, MCF-7 and GM12878 cells.

% Note: the 2015 buenrostro paper had chemical perturbations. Perturbation of K562 cells with cell-cycle inhibitors showed to reduced variability at cell-cycle-linked regions. JUN inhibition specifically increased JUN-associated region variability, and Imatinib selectively affected G1/S-related sites. In GM12878 cells, TNF-\textalpha{} stimulation elevated variability at NF-\textkappaB target sites.

One disadvantage of the CRISPR screening methods above is that most of the vectors described do not contain Cas9, limiting the studies to transgenic and immortal cell lines. Most perturbation studies so far also deal with CRISPRa/i, CRISPR knockouts, but not yet with gene expression constructs (for example: ectopic TF expression). Such studies are considerably more difficult to implement in a pooled manner, since transcript length has an effect on transfection, packaging and transduction efficiency, which in turn biases MOI and dosage. These effects call for the titration of viral concentrations and arrayed screening, which is expensive and laborious.

Single-cell RNA sequencing have recently also been used in chemical screens. For example, application of sci-RNA-seq on \num{5000} wells with 188 different small molecules $\times$ four doses $\times$ duplicates $\times$ 3 cell lines showed that \textit{MEK} inhibitors affect \textit{KRAS}/\textit{ABL}-mutant A549 and K562 cells, but not MCF-7\sjcitep[.]{srivatsan2020massively} 17 different HDAC inhibitors each shared a transcriptional trajectory, arresting cell-cycling signaling. Moreover, the large number of cells assessed (\num{649340}) allowed per-condition cell counts to be aggregated into pseudo-viability scores. More recently, an extremely large study applied Parse Biosciences' GigaLab SPLiT-seq to profile 379 drugs across 50 genetically diverse human cancer cell lines, capturing over 95.6 million single-cell transcriptomes (median \num{1287} cells per condition, median \num{1890} transcripts per cell) across \num{52886} unique cell line-drug-dose combinations. All cell lines were grown together to form "mosaic tumor" spheroids in mixed cultures. The screen, a collaboration between Arc institute and Vevo Therapeutics, revealed pronounced transcriptional shifts in response to proteasome, HDAC, PI3K/AKT, and CDK inhibitors, and confirmed genotype-specific responses to RAS/RAF pathway inhibitors like Dabrafenib and RMC-6236.
