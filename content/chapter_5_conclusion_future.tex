\chapter{Conclusion and future perspectives}
The work described here encompasses two parts. First, we developed an open-source droplet microfluidic platform for scATAC-seq and scRNA-seq, and second, we benchmarked this assay together with other open-source and commercial protocols. Together, these efforts touched on all low-level aspects of omics data generation: we optimized reaction chemistries, engineered hardware, wrote software, managed large data structures, and coordinated high-power compute. Overall, the work fits in a broader trend of generating large datasets explicitly for model training. This strategy first began to show results in games like go and chess\sjcitep[,]{sutton2019bitterlesson} but has recently manifested in more tangible applications such as self-driving cars\sjcitep[,]{waymo2025scaling} and language models\sjcitep[.]{brown2020languagemodelsfewshotlearners} Exploring the parameter sensitivity ("scaling laws") of language model training has shown that their performance continues to scale with model size, dataset size and training length - although model size had the greatest impact for LLMs\sjcitep[.]{kaplan2020scalinglawsneurallanguage} For example, Google's BERT was trained on just \SI{16}{\giga\byte} of uncompressed text\sjcitep[,]{devlin2019bertpretrainingdeepbidirectional} whereas OpenAI's GPT-3 was trained on \textasciitilde{300B} tokens sourced from Common Crawl, Wikipedia, and nearly all books, for a total of \textasciitilde{\SI{700}{\giga\byte}} of compressed data\sjcitep[.]{brown2020languagemodelsfewshotlearners} Compute was scaled similarly, roughly \SI{1.2e17}{\flops} versus \SI{3.14e23}{\flops}. While both models are distinct in architecture and intent, performance differences are evident. It can thus be posed that GPT-3's performance jump was largely caused by a disproportionally large commitment of compute (which allowed larger data to be used). Later improvements were made with architectural novelties like mixture of experts (MoE) and reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF)\sjcitep[]{fedus2022switchtransformersscalingtrillion,ouyang2022traininglanguagemodelsfollow}
