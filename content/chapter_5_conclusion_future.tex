\chapter{Conclusion and future perspectives}
The work described here encompasses two parts. First, we developed an open-source droplet microfluidic platform for scATAC-seq and scRNA-seq, and second, we benchmarked this assay together with other open-source and commercial protocols. These efforts touched on all low-level aspects of omics data generation: we optimized reaction physico-chemistries, engineered hardware, wrote software, and coordinated large data structures and compute resources. Overall, we gained and shared insights along this chain, leading to better open-source infrastructure for the scientific community.

The availability of cheap predictive tools helps advance science in a number of ways. First and foremost, good models save costs by narrowing down the number of hypotheses. Synthetic enhancer candidates, for example, can be evaluated in-silico, filtering the most promising ones to evaluate in the reporter cohort. These reductions in cost also reduce the barrier to entry, distributing work more efficiently, and improving iteration speed\sjcitep[.]{lakhani2007principles}

Since ML models value negative and control data, their use incentivizes data sharing and transparency, and potentially the repurposing of existing databases.

There are three main areas: the first, and the most logical continuations on this work specifically, is the effect of genetic perturbations on the regulome. As shown in chapter [x], Perturbation screens have already been used as a tool for causative inference. The resulting models can be used to help understand disease ethiology from a genomic or regulomic point of view. Second is cellular response to small molecules in drug design. While medicinal chemists make use of some modeling tools, most work is still performed using brute force screens, intelligent design, and iterative campaigns. Finally, and perhaps the "holy grail" of regulomics, is deterministic modulation of cellular differentiation processes. Using predictive models, we should ultimately be able to develop differentiation processes for various cell types.
