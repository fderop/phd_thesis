\chapter{\nohyphens{Introduction}}
The following introductory chapters will attempt to build the foundation for the explorations that follow by providing a historical overview of chromatin accessibility based on historical accounts\sjcitep[,]{slackConradHalWaddington2002, olinsChromatinHistoryOur2003, cobbWhoDiscoveredMessenger2015, deichmannChromatinItsHistory2015, deichmannEpigeneticsOriginsEvolution2016, lis50YearHistory2019, roeder50YearsEukaryotic2019} and frame it within the ongoing research on decoding gene regulation. Then, we illustrate the state of the art of single-cell chromatin sequencing technologies when I began this work, and delve into the capabilities and limitations of several breakthrough techniques. This context serves to highlight the importance of the subject matter, and to motivate our subsequent discussions and findings. Finally, machine learning advances in various -omics will be briefly discussed.

\section{Pioneers}
\label{sect:early}
The word "cell" was first used in 1665 by Robert Hooke to describe the hexagonal walls he observed in a thin slice of cork through a self-made microscope\sjcitep[.]{hookeMicrographia1665} It took several more centuries of research until the concept of cells as the foundational building blocks of life began to take shape. Between 1817 and 1826, Baltic-German polymath and explorer Karl Ernst von B\"{a}r made the first accounts of egg cells, development of the blastula, the notochord and the three germ layers. Only in 1839, Theodor Schwann proposed that animals, just like plants, were composed of cells\sjcitep[,]{schwannMikroskopischeUntersuchungenUber1839} and Rudolf Virchow and Robert Remak later added that cells were the product of division of pre-existing cells\sjcitep[.]{rudolfCellularpathologieIhrerBegrundung1858} While studying this process of division in 1879, Walther Flemming first used the term "chromatin" to describe the filament-like threads between two dividing cells in 1879\sjcitep[.]{flemmingBeitrageZurKenntniss1879} The term, derived from the Greek {\greekfont χρῶμ{\greekfont α}} (\textit{chroma}, color) referred to the filaments' tendency to absorb dyes. Flemming's observations did not differ much from his colleague Wilhelm Waldeyer's description of "chromosomes"\llap{.}\sjcitep[]{deichmannChromatinItsHistory2015} Both of these early findings directly stemmed from the technological refinement of microscopic lenses, which had by now become accurate enough to permit detailed studies of developing embryos. This early area of cell biology produced a number of fundamental questions that would engage the minds of biologists to this day:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
	\item How can a single fertilised egg cell divide and self-organise into an entire organism?
	\item Where is the information that steers this process encoded, and how is it interpreted?
	\item How can this process go wrong, leading to disease, and can we manipulate it?
\end{enumerate}

At that time, the concept of physical store and transfer of genetic information was not yet widely accepted, and the function and identity of Flemming and Waldeyer's chromosomes was still unknown. In search of answers, marine embryologist Thomas Hunt Morgan began studying inheritance in \textit{Drosophila melanogaster} (fruit fly) in 1908. After years of breeding flies, a mutant with white eyes (aptly named \textit{white}) appeared. Through careful tracking, Morgan discovered that these pigmentless eyes followed a sex-dependent inheritance pattern which could be reliably predicted across generations\sjcitep[.]{morganSexLimitedInheritance1910} More than half a century earlier, Moravian friar Gregor Mendel had demonstrated the exact same principles in \textit{Pisum sativum} (sweet pea), but his work was initially ignored and later disputed by the scientific community upon its rediscovery in 1900. By 1909, however, Danish botanist Wilhelm Johannsen had experimentally repeated much of Mendel's work, and coined the term "gene" to describe the abstract "unit of heredity" that is used to pass on traits between generations\sjcitep[.]{johannsen1909erblichkeitslehre}

Morgan, together with his undergraduate student Alfred Sturtevant, discovered that co-inheritance of genes also followed reproducible patterns ("linkage analysis")\sjcitep[.]{sturtevant1913} This finding supported the idea that genes were located on a linear carrier. A second breakthrough came when Morgan demonstrated that the chromosomes observed by Flemming and Waldeyer were in fact the physical bodies responsible for the transfer of phenotypic traits\sjcitep[.]{morganRandomSegregationCoupling1911} Armed with experimental evidence gathered from thousands of generations of fruit flies, Morgan formally unified the theory of Mendelian inheritance with the physical transfer of genes on chromosomes in 1915\sjcitep[.]{morganMechanismMendelianHeredity1915}

Together, Morgan's findings inaugurated the 20\textsuperscript{th} century's era of genetics, and defined the context for many of the major discoveries that ensued. Hermann Muller, for example, discovered the mutagenic effects of irradiation in \textit{Drosophila}\sjcitep[,]{Muller1927} accelerating the study of mutations\sjcitep[.]{muller1930} In fact, Muller showed that relocation of genes, inferred through Morgan and Sturtevant's linkage analysis, could alter their "variegation" (expression), a very early understanding of cis gene regulation!

In parallel, the field of embryology began to consolidate. Early views such as "preformationism"\llap{,} the idea that all parts of the body were present in miniature form in the embryo at conception, were defeated when Hans Spemann began constricting dividing \textit{Triturus} (newt) embryos. Both halves of the embryo could develop into full, though smaller, animals \textit{if} part of the dorsal side of the embryo was present. Later, Spemann and his student Hilde Mangold, discovered that transplantation of the dorsal lip from one embryo to the ventral side of another embryo would induce the formation of a ventrally conjoined twin (\autoref{fig:spemannmangold})\sjcitep[.]{spemannUberInduktionEmbryonenanlagen1924} Importantly, the pair showed that the conjoined secondary embryo consisted of tissue from the \textit{host}, indicating that the transplant merely instructed its host surroundings to change developmental fate. Spemann and Mangold later named this steering tissue the "organizer field"\llap{,} and multiple labs attempted (unsuccessfully) to purify and characterise the compounds responsible for these processes in an "organizer gold rush"\llap{.}\sjcitep[]{slackConradHalWaddington2002}

However, the link between embryonic development and the "new" insights in genetics was not readily made, even disputed by parts of the scientific community. This sentiment began to change after a young Conrad Waddington, who had previously been performing organizer grafting experiments in mammals and birds, visited Morgan's fly lab in 1935. Here, Waddington discovered that specific heritable genetic mutations could disrupt \textit{Drosophila} wing development at specific stages\sjcitep[.]{waddingtonGeneticControlWing1940} Based on these findings, Waddington wrote his seminal work \textit{Organisers and Genes} in 1940, and introduced the notion that embryonic development was a large decision tree under the control of genes, which he metaphorically described as a "landscape"\llap{.}\sjcitep[]{waddingtonOrganisersGenes1940, waddingtonStrategyGenes1957} A ball, representing a cell's current state, metaphorically rolls down the furrows in the landscape, passing several bifurcations which ultimately determine its fate (\autoref{fig:waddington_landscape}a). Beneath the surface, a series of tension wires, symbolizing genes, forcefully pull the surface into shape (\autoref{fig:waddington_landscape}b). Disruptions in an organism's genetic context, for example by mutation, could alter the landscape and thus the cell's final state.

It must be noted that the genes Morgan and Waddington addressed were still somewhat vaguely-defined, as their exact physical nature beyond the chromosome was still unknown. Phoebus Levene, who had identified adenine, guanine, thymine, cytosine, deoxyribose-phosphate backbone as the basic components of DNA, suggested that DNA was ordered in tandem tetranucleotide repeats\sjcitep[.]{levene1919structure} In fact, the storage of genetic information was attributed to proteins at the time, as they made up a much larger portion of the chromosomal dry mass than nucleic acids (DNA, RNA), and were known to be more complex in composition. This notion was overturned in 1944, when Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty demonstrated that it was transfer of acids, and not proteins, purified from a non-virulent strain of bacteria that could "transform" a virulent strain to the non-virulent strain\sjcitep[.]{griffithSignificancePneumococcalTypes1928,averyStudiesChemicalNature1944} Despite uncertainties about the physical nature of genetic information, awareness of the link between disease phenotypes, proteins and genes began to rise. For example sickle cell anemia, which was known to follow a Mendelian, heterozygous-recessive inheritance pattern, was shown to be caused by an aberrant form of hemoglobin in red blood cells. Since hemoglobin is a protein, and people with the trait had a 50:50 mix, the most logical explanation was that the gene must encode the structure of the protein\sjcitep[,]{pauling1949sickle} for the first time showing \textit{"a clear case of a change produced in a protein molecule by an allelic change in a single gene"}. A major breakthrough in the molecular understanding of genetic information came when Watson, Crick, and Franklin resolved DNA's double-helix structure using X-ray crystallography\sjcitep[,]{watsonMolecularStructureNucleic1953} and for the first time suggested that \textit{"the precise sequence of the bases is the code which carries the genetical information"}\sjcitep[.]{,watsonGeneticalImplicationsStructure1953} It had thus become clear that life stores information in the nucleotide sequence of DNA.

Although Morgan and Waddington's visions of genes as decision makers for a cell's behaviour were largely theoretical, they were soon substantiated. Jacques Monod, also a former student of Thomas Hunt Morgan, observed that bacteria grown in a medium containing both glucose and lactose would preferentially feed on glucose, and would only begin feeding on lactose once the other had run out. Further research together with Fran\c{c}ois Jacob showed that the production of {\greekfont β}-galactosidase, the enzyme responsible for this metabolic switch, was under the control of a "repressor"\llap{,} which was inactivated by the presence of lactose. Together, the pair published these findings in 1961\sjcitep[,]{jacobGeneticRegulatoryMechanisms1961} in an article which features the first diagram of a \textit{gene regulatory network} (\autoref{fig:lac_repressor}). The Jacob-Monod diagram also explicitly showed the repressor as a protein originating from a gene. Genes thus (indirectly) controlled other genes, and therefore the behaviour of the cell - precisely how Conrad Waddington had prophesised twenty years prior.

Earlier that month, Jacob, together with Sidney Brenner, had also proposed the existence of an unstable form of RNA which acted as a "messenger" to transfer information from DNA-based genes to proteins\sjcitep[.]{brennerUnstableIntermediateCarrying1961} This messenger RNA was already clearly depicted as the intermediate carrier of information between genes and proteins in Jacob \& Monod's gene regulatory network. The exact code through which the sequence of mRNA translates to proteins was deciphered soon after using clever "forward" experiments: Marshall Nirenberg fed synthetic RNA homopolymer repeats (e.g. UUU\textsubscript{n}, AAA\textsubscript{n}, ...) to cell-free protein synthesis systems in which one of the 20 possible amino-acids were radioactively labeled. The detection of a radioactive protein product then revealed the identity of the amino-acid for which the input homopolymer coded\sjcitep[.]{nirenbergRNACodewordsProtein1964} Har Gobind Khorana combined more complex RNA trinucleotide repeat polymers (e.g. ACA\textsubscript{n}, UAG\textsubscript{n}, ...) with chemical analysis of the resulting proteins to decipher the remaining possible 3-nucleotide codons, producing a finalised code cipher in 1966\sjcitep[.]{khoranaPolynucleotideSynthesisGenetic1966} These experiments together confirmed how genes encoded for proteins.

Precise extraction and purification of specific proteins or other macromolecules began to take a center stage. For example, in 1966 Gilbert and M{\"u}ller-Hill for the first time purified \textit{lac} repressor protein fractions, confirming Jacob and Monod's theoretical model of a proteins acting as repressors\sjcitep[.]{gilbert1966} A year later, in a letter titled "\textit{The Lac Operator is DNA}"\llap{,} the pair showed that the the \textit{lac} repressor specifically binds to DNA extracted from {\greekfont {\greekfont {\greekfont {\greekfont λ}}}}{}80 phage carrying the \textit{lac} operon, and that this interaction could be disrupted by exposing the complex to IPTG\sjcitep[.]{gilbert1967}

While codons had been de-coded, a "reverse" experiment in which the sequence of a given DNA or RNA molecule could be sequenced had not been performed. Walter Fiers (Ghent University) was the first to do so in 1972 by reporting a complete sequence of the bacteriophage MS2 coat protein gene\sjcitep[.]{jouNucleotideSequenceGene1972} Fiers first digested the phage's \textsuperscript{32}P-labeled RNA genome using ribonuclease T\textsubscript{1}, which cuts RNA at G positions, and iteratively separated the resulting fragments based on size and charge through multiple rounds of two-dimensional fractionation. Jo\"{e}l Vandekerckhove and Marc Van Montagu (both also Ghent University) had simultaneously been working on determining the amino-acid sequence of the MS2 coat protein\sjcitep[.]{vandekerckhoveUseFluorescamineSequence1973} Under the guidance of Van Montagu's preliminary amino-acid map and the Nirenberg-Khorana codon dictionary, Fiers and colleagues were able to puzzle together their fragments into a near-complete base sequence of the MS2 coat protein gene.

Finally, in 1973, Maxam \& Gilbert sequenced the \textit{lac} operator DNA, fully cementing Jacob \& Monod's early model of gene regulation\sjcitep[.]{gilbert1973nucleotide} Restriction enzymes with known cut sites were used to digest a purified RNA copy of the operon, and the fragments were separated on a two-dimensional PAGE developed by Fiers and De Wachter\sjcitep[.]{dewachter1972} Fragment lengths generated by different restriction enzymes could be used to infer the 27 bp-long sequence: \textit{5'-TGG\textbf{AATTGT}G\textbf{A}G\textbf{C}G\textbf{G}A\textbf{T}A\textbf{ACAATT}-3'}. Gilbert and Maxam remarked that the palindromic symmetry in this short motif could imply that the sequence is bound by a dimer or tetramer of \textit{lac} repressor proteins. Later, they published an improved DNA sequencing method which relied on comparing the 4 electrophoresis ladders generated by chemically cleaving the DNA of interest at G, C, G+A or C+T sites\sjcitep[.]{maxamGilbert1977} The first exhaustive DNA sequencing method was thus a direct consequence of gene regulation research.

Soon after, Frederick Sanger, who had developed the two-dimensional fractionation used by Fiers\sjcitep[,]{sangerTwodimensionalFractionationProcedure1965} and had already been awarded the Nobel prize for the amino-acid sequence of insulin in 1958, succeeded in deterministically sequencing the entire DNA genome of bacteriophage {\greekfont {\greekfont φ}} X174 in 1977\sjcitep[.]{sangerDNASequencingChainterminating1977} Earlier work had shown that dideoxy variants of the four common nucleotides (A, C, G \& T) would inhibit further polymerization when included into the newly synthesised DNA strand due to the absence of a reactive 3'-OH. Sanger devised that a polymerization reaction performed in a mixture of the four common nucleotides combined with a single dideoxy variant will produce a mixture of strands terminated at each position of the dideoxy variant. He further reasoned that performing this reaction for all four dideoxy variants in parallel should yield all the positions of each of the four bases (\autoref{fig:sangersequencing}). Indeed, when applied to fragments of {\greekfont {\greekfont φ}} X174 DNA, the entire phage's genome (\num{5386} base pairs) could be reconstructed. Initially, Maxam-Gilbert sequencing was more popular than Sanger's method because it could use purified DNA directly without cloning for template amplification, but the toxicity of hydrazine, dimethyl sulfate and radioactive \textsuperscript{32}P ultimately prevented commercialisation and adoption. Sanger sequencing was commercialised throughout the 80s, first as a service, and later packaged into benchtop devices.

\section{DNA-binding proteins}
While the function of chromatin's DNA component as the primary store of information had become clear, the relationship between its protein content and nucleic acids was still under dispute. Alfred and Ellen Stedman investigated different histone fractions between different species and tissues, and proposed that \textit{"the basic proteins of cell nuclei are gene inhibitors, each histone or protamine being capable of suppressing the activities of specific groups of genes"}\sjcitep[.]{stedman1951} This idea was confirmed experimentally when purified, protein-free DNA reconstituted with histone proteins was shown to be inactive in RNA synthesis, despite presence of RNA polymerase\sjcitep[.]{huang1962} Further study of this inhibitory capacity found that \textit{"relatively minor modifications of histone structure (acetylation), taking place on the intact protein molecule, offer a means of switching on or off RNA synthesis at different loci along the chromosome"}\sjcitep[.]{allfrey1964}

By the early 1970s, biochemists had chemically identified five different histone proteins (H1, H2A, H2B, H3, H4), present at ratios of 0.5:1:1:1:1. Histones H2A and H2B were found to be rich in lysine, and H3 in arginine, positively charged amino acids known to bind DNA through electrostatic attraction\sjcitep[.]{delange1972} X-ray diffraction research suggested a DNA "super-helix" structure stabilized by histone proteins\sjcitep[,]{pardon1972} while others found "thin fibrils" and "nodular elements"\llap{.}\sjcitep[]{slayter1972} It was however known that staphylococcal nuclease would cleave rat liver DNA into fragments of multiples of 200 base pairs in length, heavily hinting that DNA and histone proteins were organised in discrete units\sjcitep[.]{hewish1973} This hypothesis was strengthened by early electron microscopy, which directly visualized chromatin fibers as linear arrays of particles \textasciitilde{\SI{70}{\angstrom}} in diameter resembling "beads on a string"\llap{.}\sjcitep[]{olins1974spheroid}

Finally, in 1974, Kornberg and Thomas showed that histone proteins H2A and H2B form dimers, and H3 and H4 formed stable tetramers\sjcitep[.]{kornbergChromatinStructureOligomers1974} When mixed with DNA, these dimers and tetramers returned X-ray diffraction spectra identical to native chromatin. Later research with enhanced resolution X-ray diffraction clearly showed a repeating pattern with a periodicity of \textasciitilde{\SI{100}{\angstrom}}\sjcitep[.]{kornbergchromatinstructurerepeating1974a} Kornberg remarked that \textit{"the occurrence of multiples rather than just 200 base pair pieces is presumably due to some cleavage sites being blocked by nonhistone proteins"} - a prescient vision of transcription factors. Histone protein H1 was later identified as a stabilizer, binding to 20-80 bp long DNA links between histone octamers, leading to supercoiling and further chromatin compaction\sjcitep[.]{finch1976} This new "nucleosome" model of chromatin compacts DNA \textasciitilde{7-fold} at the nucleosome level, and further supercoiling explains how mitotic chromosomes achieve a 10 000-fold compaction. Later, Weintraub and Groudine showed that actively expressed genes are preferentially digested by DNase I\sjcitep[.]{weintraub1976chromosomal} Moreover, digestion with staphylococcal nuclease did \textit{not} show preference for active genes, suggesting that active genes are still nucleosome-bound, although more loosely.

By the late 70s, several pieces of the gene regulatory puzzle had been discovered:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
	\item Genetic information is stored in the base sequence of a cell's DNA.
	\item This information is transferred to proteins according to a well-defined codon "dictionary" through messenger RNA.
	\item Some proteins, such as the \textit{lac} repressor, are capable of influencing transcription and thus the behaviour of the cell.
\end{enumerate}

However, mechanistic and molecular insights in precisely \textit{how} genes might be regulated molecularly were still sparse. Several breakthroughs came from the lab of Robert Roeder, who had previously purified the polymerase enzymes that catalyze the transcription of messenger RNA from DNA in 1969\sjcitep[.]{roederMultipleFormsDNAdependent1969} His lab showed that purified RNA polymerases could only initiate the transcription of DNA templates in the presence of uncharacterised cellular protein extracts. This finding confirmed earlier ideas that additional, then-unknown factors were necessary to initiate the transcription reaction\sjcitep[.]{ngTranscriptionClonedXenopus1979} Several such initiation factors were purified and characterised within the next year\sjcitep[,]{matsuiMultipleFactorsAre1980} and it soon transpired that a large number of different (co-)factors are necessary for transcription to occur. Parallel research led to the discovery of a protein which induced expression of the \textit{5S} ribosomal RNA gene in \textit{Xenopus} (clawed frog) egg cells. This factor, later named Transcription Factor III (\textit{TFIIIa}), was the first protein shown to initiate transcription of a \textit{specific} gene, rather than genome wide, in eukaryotes. Roeder suggested that \textit{TFIIIa} might \textit{"recognize a unique region of DNA"}, a hypothesis which was explored and demonstrated soon after when \textit{TFIIIa} was shown to bind precisely on position 45 to 96 of the \textit{5S} gene\sjcitep[.]{engelkeSpecificInteractionPurified1980} \textit{5S} rRNA, the gene enhanced by Roeder's original transcription factor, \textit{TFIIIa}, is ubiquitously present in all cells across all known organisms. Transcription factors involved in more specialised, cell-type specific processes were identified and characterised soon after. Examples include the glucocorticoid receptor \textit{Gr}\sjcitep[,]{payvarPurifiedGlucocorticoidReceptors1981} \textit{Sp1}\sjcitep[,]{dynanIsolationTranscriptionFactors1983} and \textit{Gal4}\sjcitep[,]{bramSpecificProteinBinding1985} factors which are still under scrutiny to this day. It also became clear that some of these factors were extremely powerful: their ectopic expression could convert one cell type to another\sjcitep[,]{davisExpressionSingleTransfected1987} and later specific combinations were found to even revert fully differentiated skin cells back to stem cells\sjcitep[.]{takahashiInductionPluripotentStem2006} The molecules responsible for Spemann \& Mangold's organizer were hypothesized to be transcription factors, and several of them were identified molecularly shortly after\sjcitep[.]{choMolecularNatureSpemann1991, lambNeuralInductionSecreted1993}

In parallel, understanding of gene regulation was shifting from an exclusively promoter-proximal process (e.g. \textit{TFIIIa}, TATA-box) to also include promoter-distal elements. Reporter studies by Banerji and colleagues showed that a 72 bp tandem repeat sequence located 200 bp upstream of the SV40 early promoter, which was known to reduce nearby gene expression when mutated, could drive expression at large distances\sjcitep[.]{banerji1981expression} Constructs containing the 72 bp repeat, \textit{"for convenience referred to as the enhancer"}, showed a 200-fold increase in reporter ({\greekfont β}-globin) expression, and removal of this specific sequence ablated the effect. Moreover, inversion of the region relative to the reporter gene conserved expression, and this effect remained when inserted at distances of up to \SI{3.3}{\kilo\base}. Finally, co-transfection of separate plasmids - one with the SV40 72 bp enhancer, the other containing the {\greekfont β}-globin reporter gene - did not show expression, confirming that the SV40 enhancer works in cis only. Further research on the immunoglobulin (\textit{Ig}) enhancer, which specifically drives expression of \textit{recombined} V(D)J genes only, showed that this enhancer was active only in B-lymphocyte myeloma cells, not in HeLa, 3T6, or mink lung cells - cell-type specific enhancer activity!\sjcitep[]{banerji1983lymphocyte} The group also correctly proposed that the cell-type specific nature of the Ig enhancer owes its effect to cell-type dependent expression of its binding transcription factors, and that that chromosomal translocations could lead to oncogenic expression such as observed in Burkitt's lymphoma.

Together with the new discoveries of genomic cis-regulatory elements, an explosion in discovery of the physics behind protein-DNA-binding mechanisms occurred. Crystallography of {{\greekfont {\greekfont {\greekfont {\greekfont λ}}}}{}} phage (\textit{cI}), \textit{Cro} and \textit{E. coli} \textit{cap}, three known DNA-binding proteins, showed the first generalized mechanism of binding: two {{\greekfont α}{}}-helices joined by a tight turn (helix-turn-helix motif) generate a sharp nick that fits into the major groove of DNA, leading to sequence-specific - though degenerate - binding\sjcitep[.]{pabo1984protein} Simultaneous studies in \textit{D. melanogaster} led to the discovery of the homeodomain, a 60 amino acid DNA-binding domain highly conserved across metazoans, especially those with segmented body plans\sjcitep[.]{mcginnis1984homologous} Later, it was shown that the homeodomain also uses a helix-turn-helix structure, but is significantly longer and complexes DNA differently than the {\greekfont {\greekfont {\greekfont {\greekfont λ}}}}{} HTH\sjcitep[.]{kissinger1990engrailed} Zinc-binding repeating Cys2His2 motifs were further identified in \textit{TFIIIa}, and these zinc-protein complexes were also shown to bind specific DNA motifs\sjcitep[.]{miller1985repetitive, pavletich1991zinc} Next, an amino acid motif conserved across transcription factors \textit{CEBP}, \textit{Myc}, \textit{Fos}, \textit{Jun} and the yeast transcription factor \textit{GCN4} were shown to interdigitate through leucine residues every seventh position ("basic leucine zipper"). This interdigitation allows these factors to dimerize, and bind palindromic DNA motifs through adjacent DNA-recognizing residues\sjcitep[.]{landschulz1988leucine} Finally, basic helix-loop-helix motifs were shown to similarly dimerize, allowing both monomers and hetero/homodimers to recognize and bind the palindromic E-box motif (consensus \verb|CANNTG|, but ideally \verb|CACGTG|)\sjcitep[.]{murre1989interactions, ellenberger1994crystal} Structural studies also confirmed that the \textit{lac} repressor is a tetramer protein capable of binding two operator sites simultaneously, forming DNA loops that repress expression\sjcitep[.]{lewis1996}

% 1977: PhiX/sanger, 5386 bases
While mechanistic insights into gene regulation began to form in the 1980s, precise knowledge of gene sequences in human was still sparse. Cystic fibrosis, the most common lethal autosomal recessive disease among Caucasians, was \textit{suspected} to be monogenic based on its Mendelian inheritance pattern, but the precise gene responsible for the disease had not been identified. By 1985, the location of the gene was narrowed down to a region \textit{"1 percent of the human genome, about 30 million base pairs"} in size\sjcitep[.]{tsui1985} This result was achieved using the linkage analysis methods previously invented by Morgan \& Sturtevant, combined with restriction fragment length polymorphisms (RFLPs)\sjcitep[,]{botstein1980} requiring thousands of clone library Southern blots. Sequencing the \textit{CFTR} gene by Sanger's method took four more years\sjcitep[,]{riordan1989,rommens1989} revealing the deletion of three nucleotides (\textit{{{\greekfont δ}}F508}) was the disease's most common causal mutation. This marked the first time a disease's causal gene was identified by genomics bottom-ups genomics before its protein was known. HTT, the gene responsible for Huntington's disease, was identified in a similar way and timeframe\sjcitep[.]{gusella1983polymorphic,macdonaldNovelGeneContaining1993}

The decade-long efforts required to identify CFTR and HTT led to widespread consensus in the academic community: a complete reference sequence of the human genome was necessary. With a human genome available, researchers would be able to immediately compare any extracted DNA to a healthy reference and locate disease polymorphisms rapidly. Moreover, it had become canon that genomes contained information beyond protein-coding genes, and that understanding the relationship between these different genomic elements and disease would lead to transformative medicine. Francis Collins, who had been heavily involved in the CFTR gene hunt, became a leading advocate for the Human Genome Project\sjcitep[,]{collins1999} which was officially kicked off in 1990. Originally a joint academic effort between the US National Institutes of Health, several international collaborators (United Kingdom, Germany, Japan, France) soon followed\sjcitep[.]{birney2021international} Interestingly, the US Department of Energy also played a major role, with Lawrence Livermore, Los Alamos and Lawrence Berkeley labs providing extensive support in computational analysis and data storage.

The project was first expected to take 15 years and cost 3 billion dollars. Early efforts focused on a hierarchical clone-by-clone approach: the genome was broken into fragments (\textasciitilde\SI{200}{\kilo\base}) and cloned into over \num{100000} bacterial artificial chromosomes (BACs). These BACs were then linked into large contigs using a variety of techniques (mostly sequence tagging sites PCR, which was somewhat novel at the time\sjcitep[,]{saiki1985,mullis1986} and restriction enzyme fingerprinting), which were in turn mapped to entire chromosomes. A minimum tile path map, dividing the genome into regions that were distinct enough to sequence by Sanger, was made. Individual BAC clones were then further fragmented into smaller \textasciitilde\SI{1000}{\bp} pieces which could be sequenced individually. The first several years of the project were spent on the minimum-tiling BAC generation and archival. Repetitive DNA regions and coverage gaps complicated the assembly of contigs, and Sanger's dideoxy method was incredibly labour intensive. Luckily, automation soon followed. One company played a particularly important early role: ABI. First by releasing the ABI Prism 373 and 377, then the ABI Prism 310 capillary gel electrophoresis machine, and finally the ABI 3700 96-plex capillary gel electrophoresis analyzer, which became the workhorse of the human genome project. Ensuing throughput advances were staggering: whereas automated slab-gel instruments produced \num{1000} bp/day in 1985, the ABI 3700 capillary array reached \num{1000} bp/s by 1998, and in 1999, HGP centers were sequencing \textasciitilde90 Gbp per month.

Directly shearing the genome into sequencing-ready fragments, the "shotgun" strategy, had previously been used to manually map the kilobase-sized genomes of several bacteriophages. This method was deemed unsuitable for larger genomes due to the sheer number of reads required, an amount of data that would not have been possible to align on early 90s computing infrastructure. However, in 1995, Craig Venter's group at the Institute for Genomic Research managed to sequence the 1.8 MB \textit{H. influenzae} genome using the shotgun approach\sjcitep[.]{fleischmann1995whole} By cloning randomly sheared chromosomal fragments directly, and sequencing the resulting plasmids, the virus's entire genome was sequenced in just 14 months. \num{19687} clones generated \num{24304} reads ($6.3\times$ coverage), taking 30 hours to assemble on the 200 MHz SPARC supercomputer. The assembled genome revealed \textasciitilde\num{1750} genes, a third of which lacked any known function. Improvements in computing infrastructure and sequencing hardware had made the shotgun approach viable for larger genomes, and in 1998, Venter launched Celera Genomics in partnership with Perkin-Elmer/ABI. The group announced a plan for whole-genome shotgun sequencing of the 3.2 Gb human genome using 300 ABI 3700 capillary sequencers and a 500-processor Compaq Alpha compute cluster\sjcitep[.]{venter1998shotgun} In parallel, Celera further refined their strategy, completing the \textit{D. melanogaster} genome in 2000 - although heavily leaning on a partial (29 Mb of the 120 Mb) public reference genome published by international consortia\sjcitep[.]{adams2000genome} By this time, the HGP consortium also began applying shotgun sequencing to its continuing effort. Both the HGP and Celera assemblies of the human genome - each approximately 90\% complete - were released in 2001\sjcitep[.]{landerinitialsequencinganalysis2001,venter2001sequence} Alignment took several months of compute. Of course, the Human Genome Project had been releasing intermittent drafts which Celera could freely download from GenBank. Celera's final assembly was composed of 27M shotgun reads plus 18M reads sourced from shredded public HGP data, and was accessible only under license. While initially financially successful, the Celera assembly was soon surpassed by the HGP's free, and eventually more accurate, version. Demand for Celera's version collapsed, and it was placed in the public domain in 2005\sjcitep[.]{kling2005future}

The human genome race cemented shotgun WGS as the default strategy for large genomes, and began an era of comparative genomics and high-throughput "omics" driven by rapidly falling sequencing costs. Its economic return was later estimated at nearly \USD800 billion on a \USD3 billion public investment, largely through the birth of sequencing-based diagnostics, bioinformatics, and high-throughput sequencing assays\sjcitep[.]{tripp2011hgpimpact} The now completed assembly allowed researchers to shift focus from recording the genome to annotating it, and finally deciphering its logic. The new fields of transcriptomics, epigenomics, metagenomics, population genetics, ... would require sequencing not just one genome, but thousands of genomes and other nucleic acid-derived samples across many species, individuals and tissues. This demand could not be met by the Sanger sequencing method, stimulating the development of a variety of new sequencing technologies.

Several NGS platforms were launched almost simultaneously: Roche/454's pyrosequencing\sjcitep[,]{margulies2005genome} ABI's SOLiD, Helicos's single-molecule chemistry, and Solexa's sequencing-by-synthesis (SBS)\sjcitep[]{bennett2005toward} each promised sequencing at a fraction of the Sanger method's cost. The competitive landscape narrowed after 2007, when Illumina acquired Solexa and began to scale SBS to industrial capacity. High signal-to-noise, coupled with better CMOS cameras and patterned flow-cells, allowed Illumina to double output roughly every 12 months while driving per-base cost down by four orders of magnitude\sjcitep[.]{metzkerSequencingTechnologiesNext2010, mardis2011decadesperspective} By 2012, benchtop instruments such as the MiSeq delivered data for \textasciitilde\USD0.10 per megabase\sjcitep[,]{shendureexpandingscopedna2012} compared with \textasciitilde\USD1000 per megabase in 2005\sjcitep[.]{shendureaccuratemultiplexpolony2005} Competing short-read platforms faded, and long-read entrants (PacBio SMRT, Oxford Nanopore) would not appear for another decade (\autoref{fig:sequencing_plaftorms}). A second wave of SBS providers (BGI/MGI, Ultima Genomics, Complete Genomics, Singular Genomics) has recently appeared, following expiry of key Illumina patents.

Sequencing by synthesis was first developed by Balasubramanian and Klenerman at Cambridge in 1998, and works by hybridizing DNA templates to an adapter oligo "lawn" on a glass flow cell\sjcitep[.]{furey1998use} Here, each individual template is amplified to generate a cluster of clones, which is then sequenced by sequentially adding fluorescently labeled nucleotides and recording where they are incorporated. Each incorporation cycle is imaged, generating imaging data of the oligo lawn. Image processing is then used to determine the position of oligo clusters present on the lawn, and these are stored in \verb|.locs| (locations) files. The basecalling algorithm will combine these \verb|.locs| files together with the imaging data to extract fluorescence intensities for all colours used per cluster. Dominant fluorescent signals then determine the base called for each cluster for every cycle. When no base can be called, \verb|N| is used. When a strand does not incorporate a base during a given cycle, or if it leads ahead by one base, it becomes out of phase with the other strands of that cluster for the rest of the sequencing run. This phasing/pre-phasing is responsible for signal deterioration further down the read, and is one of the limiting factors to maximum read length (originally \textasciitilde{30}, now up to 300 bp). Theoretical phasing rates (approximately 0.1\% of strands in a given cluster per cycle) can be used to estimate the contribution of phasing noise and deconvolve true signal from phased noise. Each basecall is assigned a "\verb|Phred|" quality score \verb|Q| based on signal-to-noise ratio, phasing, and separation of dense clusters: \( \verb|Q| = -10 \cdot \log_{10} (P) \) where \verb|P| is the probability of the basecall being correct. A \verb|Q| score of 30 corresponds to an erroneous basecall probability of 1 in 1000. The result is a binary \verb|.bcl| file, which contains the basecalls for each cluster, and can then be converted to the \verb|ASCII| \verb|.fastq| format using \verb|bcl2fastq|.

\section{Quantitative sequencing assays}
\label{sect:sequencing_assays}
Before the availability of next-generation sequencing technology, studying the genome and gene expression at scale relied heavily on microarrays. Microarrays were, and still are, used to quantify RNA transcripts\sjcitep[,]{schenaQuantitativeMonitoringGene1995} genotype samples using SNP arrays\sjcitep[,]{wang1998large} and even measure DNA-protein interactions using ChIP-on-chip\sjcitep[.]{ren2000genome} While microarrays allow for up to ~\num{50000} probes to be interrogated in a single experiment, limited dynamic range, high false-positive hybridisation and heterogeneous probe hybridisation capacities skew absolute quantification. The probe set also requires intelligent design and thus cannot discover novel sequences. Probeset size is particularly troubling for ChIP-on-Chip, since genome binning limits resolution to around 500-1000 bp - too low for novel TF motif discovery. Nonetheless, large scale, distributed application of microarray technologies culminated in a first draft of the Encyclopedia of DNA Elements (ENCODE) in 2007\sjcitep[,]{ENCODE_Project_Consortium2007-zd} a careful annotation of 30 MBs - around 1\% - of the human genome.

% The release of this draft was the last project of such scale that did not use next generation sequencing.

The now upcoming availability of NGS made it possible to bypass microarrays: specific fractions of nucleic acids extracted from tissues could be sequenced, aligned to the reference genome, and counted. This revolution led to an explosion of quantitative sequencing assays being developed. One of the first such assays was histone methylation ChIP-seq, first published in 2007\sjcitep[.]{barskiHighresolutionProfilingHistone2007} By sequencing immunoprecipitated DNA directly rather than using microarrays, methylation could now be assessed genome-wide and with fewer quantitative biases. Performing this technique for a spectrum of 20 different lys/arg histone methylation ChIP-seq variants on CD4\textsuperscript{+} T cells, and correlating the annotations with GNF SymAtlas gene expression microarray data allowed genome-wide classification and discovery of various classes of functional elements. The distribution of methylation signatures could now be systematically correlated to phenotypes, helping understand disease and development. For example, a comparison of histone methylation in mouse embryonic stem cells and embryonic fibroblasts revealed transitory methylation changes in the de-differentiation of ES\sjcitep[.]{mikkelsen2007genome}

In parallel research, ChIP-seq was also being applied to individual transcription factors at a genomic resolution of around 50 base pairs, a welcome improvement compared to microarrays. Application of ChIP-seq on neuron-restrictive silencer factor (NRSF) in Jurkat T-cells showed that the factor can also bind "weakly" to altered, non-canonical motifs dependent on local chromatin state (methylation, accessibility) and co-factor motif context\sjcitep[.]{johnsongenomewidemappingvivo2007} These findings contradicted earlier ideas of stringent binding to only canonical motifs, a rejection made possible only through the broadness of NGS. NGS also improved dynamic range compared to microarrays, showing for example that STAT1 motif enrichment was strongly correlated to absolute ChIP-seq peak height\sjcitep[.]{robertsonGenomewideProfilesSTAT12007}

% Gene-proximal (e.g. promoters) and distal (identified through comparative genomics or CpG enrichment) regulatory regions could be scanned for enrichment of known TF binding motifs sourced from databases such as TRANSFAC\sjcitep[,]{wingender2000transfac, matys2006transfac} or even parsed for de novo motif identification\sjcitep[.]{thijs2002gibbs} Significantly enriched motifs could in turn be used to reconstruct gene regulatory network candidates\sjcitep[.]{aerts2003toucan,aerts2005toucan} ready for reporter assay testing. \verb|endeavour|\sjcitep[]{aertsGenePrioritization2006} aggregated data from 8 independent databases including expression data\sjcitep[,]{barrett2005ncbi} GO terms\sjcitep[,]{ashburner2000gene} regulatory elements\sjcitep[,]{matys2006transfac} to rank gene associations with a training set (e.g. genes already known to be associated with a certain disease). Tools like these now allowed researchers to prioritized candidate genomic elements, such as novel genes, for further lab validation.

The increased genomic resolution of ChIP-seq was rapidly refining models of gene regulation, but the technique still required a strong antibody and prior knowledge of the protein or histone modification of interest. In contrast, DNase I hypersensitivity (DHS) assays could survey chromatin accessibility genome-wide, and had already been applied for genome-wide accessibility studies: first by Southern blot\sjcitep[,]{wu1980heat} then capillary Sanger sequencing\sjcitep[,]{crawford2004identifying} and later microarrays\sjcitep[.]{crawford2006dnase} Like ChIP, DNase-seq was soon translated to NGS\sjcitep[,]{boyle2008high,hesselberth2009global} rapidly contributing to even higher resolution (1 base pair) understanding of gene gene regulation. For example, matched DNase-seq and glucocorticoid receptor (GR) ChIP-seq revealed that out of \num{8236} active GR binding sites in murine mammary adenocarcinoma, 95\% were already accessible \textit{prior to} receptor activation\sjcitep[.]{john2011chromatin} These findings rejected that GR might act as a pioneer factor, remodeling inaccessible chromatin and exposing its preferred motif after receptor activation. Moreover, pituitary gland cells shared only 11.4\% of the binding sites accessible in adenocarcinoma, suggesting that differential chromatin context underlies differential responses to the same transcription factor. Unsurprisingly, ENCODE's second draft included DNase-seq data from 125 tissues, along with bisulfite sequencing, and H3K4me3 and TF ChIP-seq\sjcitep[.]{thurman2012accessible,encode2012} In total, 2.9 million DNase I hypersensitivity sites were identified and annotated genome-wide, covering almost all previously known regulatory element candidates: 94.4\% of previously known TF ChIP-seq regions were found to overlap with these DNase I sites, and only \num{3692} such sites were found in all cell types assessed, confirming that chromatin accessibility and downstream gene regulation are highly tissue and cell-type specific processes. Combination of these new regulatory readouts with GWAS further confirmed that many disease-related SNPs are located in regulatory regions, accelerating therapeutic target discovery.

By 2008, development of NGS began to displace microarrays in transcriptomics as well, first in yeast\sjcitep[,]{nagalakshmi2008transcriptional} and later in mouse\sjcitep[,]{mortazavi2008mapping} and human\sjcitep[.]{wang2008alternative} Poly(A)\textsuperscript{+} mRNA was extracted and purified from brain, liver, and skeletal muscle from C57BL/6 mice, hydrolized and reverse-transcribed to cDNA using random hexamer priming\sjcitep[.]{mortazavi2008mapping} Illumina/Solexa was used to sequence the resulting libraries. This particular study, performed by Mortazavi et al., delivered the first quantitative whole transcriptome in mouse:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
	\item \textasciitilde{\num{145000}} distinct splicing events detected, with \textasciitilde{\num{3500}} genes showing alternative splicing.
	\item \textasciitilde{\num{3000}} newly identified or extended 5' exons.
	\item \textasciitilde{\num{3000}} novel extended 3' UTRs.
	\item \textasciitilde{\num{17000}} newly annotated transcribed regions.
	\item \num{596} entirely novel candidate transcripts, including a previously unknown mir-124-1 precursor in the brain.
\end{itemize}

In addition, quantification of phage {\greekfont {\greekfont {\greekfont {\greekfont λ}}}}{} RNA spike-ins remained linear (R\textsuperscript{2} = 0.99) over 5 orders of magnitude, and correlation between technical replicates was high (R\textsuperscript{2} = 0.96). A technical comparison of RNA-seq with microarray transcriptomics showed that RNA-seq was technically superior in every aspect: RNA-seq identified 30\% more differentially expressed genes than microarrays at the same false discovery rate, and the sequencing approach was better at detecting low-abundance genes\sjcitep[.]{marioni2008rna} qPCR validation also confirmed more RNA-seq-specific findings than microarray-specific findings. Finally, \textasciitilde{\num{200000}} RNA-seq reads mapped to possible exon-exon junctions, revealing novel splicing patterns. Each RNA extract was sequenced 7 times, unsurprisingly with a Spearman correlation of 0.96 across lanes, but extraction itself was not replicated\sjcitep[.]{wang2009rna} Several major findings emerged from RNA-seq. Eukaryotic transcriptomes turned out to be more heterogeneous than anticipated. Individual papers revealed large numbers of novel transcribed regions, widespread antisense transcription, and extensive variation in 3' ends\sjcitep[.]{wang2008alternative}

Studies combining different sequencing assays also rapidly led to new insights. Application of RNA-seq, FAIRE-seq and H3K27ac/H3K27me3 ChIP-seq on melanoma directly led to understanding the gene regulatory programs underlying proliferative and invasive states\sjcitep[.]{verfaillie2015decoding} Differential gene analysis between patient biopsies from these two melanoma states showed high MITF, SOX10, and PAX3 in proliferative, and High ZEB1, SOX9 in invasive states. Similarly, FAIRE-seq showed open chromatin regions near MITF and SOX10 target genes in proliferative, and SOX9 targets in invasive states, and ChIP-seq showed high H3K27 acetylation at AP-1/TEAD target enhancers in invasive cells. Repression of TEAD in invasive cells via siRNA showed decreased cell invasion in matrigel assays and reduction in SOX9 expression, confirming TEAD as a master regulator in invasive melanoma.

By 2010, next-generation sequencing itself had become ubiquitous, but library prep still required sonication, end-repair, and adapter ligation. A breakthrough came from Tn5, an enzyme first studied decades earlier. Barbara McClintock had first observed "mutable loci" in maize arise from mobile DNA inserts\sjcitep[,]{mcclintock1950,ravindran2012} the first observation of position-effect variegation and cis-regulatory chromatin influence outside of \textit{Drosophila}\sjcitep[.]{muller1930} Two decades later, foreign DNA insertions were observed to disrupt the \textit{E. coli} \textit{gal} operon\sjcitep[.]{shapiro1969} Entire resistance plasmids were found to integrate into phage {\greekfont {\greekfont {\greekfont {\greekfont λ}}}}{}, flanked by 1.5 kb inverted repeats\sjcitep[.]{berg1975} The transposable element was finally named Tn5, a member of an entire class of transposons\sjcitep[.]{bukhari1977} By 1982, both inverted repeats, named IS50-L and IS50-R, were sequenced\sjcitep[.]{reznikoff1981,reznikoff1982} IS50-R encodes the 461 amino-acid primary transposase as well as a truncated version of the protein. This smaller protein is expressed from a second, downstream transcription start site and inhibits the complete transposase by competitive binding, repressing total transposition activity. A conserved stop codon point mutation found in the nearly identical IS50-L repeat disrupts complete expression of the transposase there, but simultaneously introduces a promotor sequence for the embedded antibiotic resistance genes. The Tn5 transposon is thus capable of transferring antibiotic resistance genes and expressing them independently of the host's endogenous mechanisms. It also self-regulates both its transposon and resistance activities. Soon, Tn5's potential as a molecular tool was recognised. For example, in-vitro Tn5 insertions could be used to disrupt genes in bacteria, with antibiotic resistance as a positive selection marker\sjcitep[.]{ruvkun1982} A first full crystallography of Tn5 protein (Tnp) in complex with DNA showed that it shares mechanistic features with HIV-1 integrase and RAG1/2 V(D)J recombinases\sjcitep[.]{davies2000threedimensional}

Owing to its use as a lab tool, Tn5 protein mutants and their molecular mechanisms were quickly studied\sjcitep[.]{wiegand1992characterization, weinreich1994evidence} A triple mutant, E54K (enhances OE binding) - M56A (disrupts inhibitor production) - L372P (increases activity by altering dimerization), was engineered to increase catalytic activity, and is the major variant in use to date ("hyperactive Tn5")\sjcitep[.]{goryshintn5vitrotransposition1998} The transposase can bind to two sites on the ends of each IS50 sequence: the inside or outside end (IE, OE). A synthetic "mosaic end" (ME), engineered by combining elements of the IE and the OE showed enhanced binding capacity, improving dimerization and complexation of Tnp with DNA\sjcitep[.]{zhou1998molecular,steinigerwhiteEvidenceUnseenTransposase2002} The transposase's exact catalytic mechanism was unknown until Bhasin and colleagues showed how the Tn5 transposase nicks at the transposon ends, forms a hairpin, releases the donor dna, and opens the hairpin and integrates at the acceptor site\sjcitep[.]{bhasin1999hairpin} A staggered cut is made at the acceptor site, and after insertion of the blunt-end transposon, the missing 9 base pairs are repaired, leading to a duplication. A classic DDE motif, known from RNAseH enzymes, in Tn5 transposase's active site coordinates magnesium ions critical for catalysis by stabilizing the DNA-protein complexes\sjcitep[.]{lovell2002twometal,reznikoffTn5ModelUnderstanding2003}

By the mid 2000s, Tn5 saw routine lab use to tag cloned DNA with selection markers, primers, or even regulatory elements\sjcitep[.]{reznikoffTn5MolecularGenetics2004} This could be achieved without in vivo expression of the transposon by loading transposase protein onto pre-cleaved DNA, and introducing the complex into electroporated bacteria. The transposase was first used by Adey and Shendure to construct shotgun sequencing libraries in 2010\sjcitep[.]{adeyRapidLowInputLowbias2010} This breakthrough greatly simplified the old workflow of fragmentation, end repair, and ligation, a process that spanned 6 to 10 hours. With Tn5 transposase "tagmentation"\llap{,} these pre-PCR steps were reduced to only 15 to 20 minutes. Moreover, the Adey protocol could use inputs as low as \SI{10}{\pico\gram} as opposed to the \SI{1}{\micro\gram} required by the ligation methods, a sensitivity increase that would later allow library extraction from single-cells.

Buenrostro and colleagues realized around 2013 that a hyper-active Tn5 dimer pre-loaded with Illumina adapters could be used not only to construct sequencing libraries, but also to interrogate the accessibility of native chromatin\sjcitep[.]{buenrostro2013transposition,buenrostro2015atacseq} Transposition was performed on intact, unfixed nuclei, with Tn5 dimers sterically hindered by occupant nucleosomes and other DNA-bound proteins. Every insertion event marks an open stretch of chromatin, and, since Tn5 already ligates sequencing adapters, subsequent PCR generates a sequencing-ready library. The resulting protocol, named ATAC-seq (assay for transposase-accessible chromatin by sequencing), required just \num{50000} cells - compared to the 1 to 100 million cells needed for DNase-seq, FAIRE-seq and ChIP-seq. Omni-ATAC, an improved protocol, introduced the triple-detergent lysis buffer (NP-40 + Tween-20 + digitonin), and optimized magnesium concentrations, dramatically reducing mitochondrial contamination and extending the assay to frozen tissue sections\sjcitep[.]{corces2017improved} These optimisations resulted in a 13-fold drop in mitochondrial reads and 3-fold rise in fraction of reads in peaks, and up to 15-fold increased number of unique fragments at similar sequencing depths.

\section{Single-cell RNA and ATAC-seq protocols}
\label{sect:single_cell_sequencing_tech}
The development of next-generation sequencing methods and quantitative -omics assays in the early 2000s - most notably RNA-seq, ChIP-seq, DNase-seq, and later ATAC-seq - allowed researchers to probe gene expression and regulatory elements in high-throughput, at genome-wide scales (\autoref{fig:sequencing_assays}). Application of these technologies revealed tissue- and cell type-specific enhancer landscapes, complex transcription factor networks, novel transcript isoforms and splicing patterns, and altered chromatin states across disease and development. Together, these bulk methods laid conceptual frameworks for understanding the genome, and significantly advanced our understanding of development, immunity, and cancer.

\begin{figure}[ht]
	\centering
	\includesvg[width=1\textwidth]{ipynb/figs/plot_pubmed.svg}

	\label{fig:sequencing_assays}

However, bulk assays cannot distinguish signals from different populations within the input sample. Unexpected heterogeneity, rare cell states, and stochastic dynamics are masked by the aggregation of thousands to millions of cells. The combination of FAC sorting and quantitative bulk sequencing methods can help, but is costly, laborious, and requires prior knowledge. Even in combination with cell-sorting, bulk assays remained unsuitable for a number of key open problems:\sjcitep[]{shapiro2013single}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
	\item Profiling of rare or transient cell-types, such as circulating tumor cells and metastatic cancer subclones.
	\item Profiling the full complexity of systems composed of potentially thousands of cell types, such as brains and the repertoire of immune cells.
	\item Reconstructing cell lineages across development, differentiation and disease progression in high temporal and potentially spatial resolution.
	\item Systematically defining cell types and identifying consensus markers, both in expression, but also in active genomic elements and modifications.
	\item Quantifying stochastic differences between cells sampled from the same type.
	\item High-throughput genetic or chemical perturbation studies.
\end{itemize}

Some of these considerations drove early exploration of gene expression at the single-cell level in the 90s, starting a decade-long quest for methods to amplify RNA material from individual cells. Eberwine and colleagues first injected an oligo-dT T7 primer and T7 reverse transcriptase directly into single rat hippocampal neurons to reverse transcribe their mRNA in situ\sjcitep[.]{eberwine1992analysis} The resulting cDNA was amplified using in vitro transcription, and relative expression levels of 10 known neuronal genes (mostly ion channels) were quantified by Southern blot. Even among \textit{morphologically similar neurons}, significant differences in gene expression were observed, recording for the first time transcriptomic heterogeneity within the same tissue. About a decade later, a double T7-based amplification method was applied to profile 12 laser microdissected rat hippocampal neurons on cDNA microarrays, revealing two new marker genes for a novel pyramidal neuron subtype\sjcitep[.]{kamme2003singlecell} Soon, PCR-based cDNA amplification strategies\sjcitep[,]{brady1993construction,brady2000expression} rather than IVT, was applied to single cells from mouse embryonic stem cells and blastomeres\sjcitep[.]{kurimoto2006improved} Mature mRNA was extracted by poly(dT) primers, reverse-transcribed and PCR-amplified, and the resulting single-cell libraries were each hybridised to \textit{individual} RNA microarrays. This revealed that the blastocyst's inner cell mass contains at least two populations: some express genes typical of primitive endoderm (\textit{Gata4}, \textit{Gata6}) and others express epiblast markers (\textit{Fgf4}, \textit{Nanog}, \textit{Klf2}).

%Radiolabeled aRNAs were also hybridised to a cDNA library extracted from NG108, a hybrid neuroblastoma-glioma cell line known to express a wide variety of neuron-associated genes. Hybridising clones were isolated and sequenced, leading to the discovery of three novel genes that did not match existing gene databases.

"True" whole transcriptome single-cell sequencing was first demonstrated in 2009 by Tang and Surani at the Gurdon Institute\sjcitep[.]{tangMRNASeqWholeTranscriptome2009} By increasing reverse transcription and PCR extension times/cycles, the group was able to amplify enough material for sequencing cDNA libraries from 6 individual mouse blastomeres. This sequencing-based approach provided a number of immediate advantages over microarray analysis:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
	\item Comparison of single-blastomere mRNA-Seq data with bulk (320 pooled cells) Affymetrix microarray data showed that \SI{94.3}{\percent} of microarray-detected genes had at least 5 transcripts in the mRNA-Seq data, but mRNA-Seq detected \num{5270} (up to 75\%) more genes in total.
	\item Sensitivity and dynamic range (\SIrange{0.2}{12000}{RPKM}) were decidedly higher for mRNA-Seq than for analog microarrays.
	\item Since near-whole transcripts were sequenced novel splice junctions could be determined. The group discovered \num{1753} novel isoforms, demonstrated for the first time that different isoforms of the same gene can be expressed in the same single cell.
\end{enumerate}

Tang et al. used the ABI SOLiD sequencer, which had become commercially available only two years prior. Since the sample extraction and amplification protocol itself were only slightly different from existing microarray protocols, it can be posed that development of the Tang method was an effect of sequencing hardware improvements. However, the new sequencing-based approach also came with a new set of challenges. Cost of the novel sequencing technology was very high - each cell in the Tang paper, sequenced at around 24 to 85 million reads, must have cost around \USD{10000} to \USD{50000} in SOLiD sequencing alone \textit{per cell}. At the time, one Affymetrix microarray sample cost in the order of \USD{800} - significantly cheaper. Despite longer extension times, the protocol was also unable to capture transcripts longer than \SI{3}{kb}, and was limited to poly(A)\textsuperscript{+} mRNA. These factors contributed to mRNA-Seq's status as a proof-of-concept technique rather than an immediate research tool.

One of the first protocols to be published after the Tang protocol was STRT-seq (Single-cell Tagged Reverse Transcription), developed by the Linnarson lab at Karolinska Institute\sjcitep[.]{islamCharacterizationSinglecellTranscriptional2011, islamHighlyMultiplexedStrandSpecific2012} The protocol applied template-switching reverse transcription (use of MMLV reverse transcriptase, which tags 5' transcripts via template switching)\sjcitep[,]{zhu2001reverse} to cells picked into individual wells, followed by pooled single-primer PCR for cDNA amplification. A total of 92 mouse cells were profiled: 48 embryonic stem cells and 44 embryonic feeder cells. Libraries were sequenced on the Illumina Genome Analyzer IIx, for a total of 110M raw reads. mRNA capture efficiency was approximately 10\%, as measured by RNA spike-in panels (ERCC). 80\% of filtered reads aligned to exonic and splice-junction features in the mouse genome, which is high even by today's standards. Introduction of degenerate bases (\verb|NNNNNNNNNN|) in the 5' adapter allowed multiple counts of PCR duplicates to be collapsed into transcript counts\sjcitep[.]{kiviojaCountingAbsoluteNumbers2011} Addition of these unique molecular adapters (UMIs) to the STRT-seq protocol, plus parallel automated cell capture on microfluidic cell capture arrays (Fluidigm C1), and oligo design optimisation improved the capture efficiency of STRT-seq from \textasciitilde{10}\% to \textasciitilde{50}\% \sjcitep[.]{islamQuantitativeSingleCell2014}

% Later on, STRT-seq was also applied to custom-milled 9600-well nano arrays, recovering between 3000 to 7500 single cells per plate and an average of \num{8706} UMIs and \num{3686} genes\sjcitep[.]{hochgerner2017strt}

% Tumor heterogeneity was already known to underlie treatment resistance\sjcitep[.]{park2010cellular} Bulk DNA sequencing and comparative genomic hybridization assays cannot identify heterogenity in mutation or copy number variation profile. Navin et al. FAC sorted 200 single cells from triple-negative breast cancer tumors, and performed whole genome amplification and sequencing on individual cells\sjcitep[.]{navin2011tumour} Due to low per-cell coverage (\textasciitilde{6\%}), copy number variations was the focus of this study. Lineage analysis identified several "bursts" of CNV shared across main subpopulations, challenging previous ideas of slow, gradual accumulation of mutations underlying cancer. Metastases were strongly enriched for aneuploid populations that represent minorities in the primary tumor, indicating that chromosomal duplication helps establish rapidly expanding metastatic clones. "By isolating, sorting and sequencing the genome of 52 cells from a primary breast tumour and 48 cells from its associated liver metastasis, Navin et al. showed that a single cell from the primary tumor mass seeded the metastatic tumour\sjcitep[.]{navinTumourEvolutionInferred2011}"

In parallel research, also at the Karolinska Institute, Ramsk\"{o}ld and Sandberg developed Smart-seq (Switching Mechanism at the 5' end of RNA Template)\sjcitep[.]{ramskoldFulllengthMRNASeqSinglecell2012} Similarly to STRT-seq, a combination of MMLV reverse-transcriptase followed by cDNA PCR was used. However, fragmentation of these full-length transcripts using Tn5 (or Covaris shearing) allowed for shotgun sequencing of the entire transcript. 42 cells from several human cancer cell lines, circulating tumor cells, and mouse oocytes were profiled. Improved isoform coverage showed that exon 13 of the NEDD4L gene, a prognosis marker, was differentially expressed between LNCaP prostate cancer and T24 bladder cancer cells. A year later, an updated Smart-seq protocol with increased sensitivity was published\sjcitep[.]{picellismartseq2sensitivefull2013} Here, the addition of a locked nucleic acid (LNA) at the TSO's 3' end and addition of betaine led to 2-4 fold cDNA yield increases. The workflow was also simplified and reduced in cost by switching to KAPA HiFi polymerase, which reduced GC-biases. The use of in-house produced recombinant Tn5\sjcitep[]{picellitn5transposasetagmentation2014} further reduced costs for Smart-seq2\sjcitep[.]{picellifulllengthrnaseqsingle2014} Researchers could now bypass Illumina's Nextera Tn5, which to this day is still enormously expensive (\textasciitilde\$\num{3600} per \SI{160}{\micro\liter}). A quantitative comparison of Smart-seq and bulk RNA-seq revealed that aggregate Smart-seq data closely resembles bulk RNA-seq data from 1 million cells, and correlates strongly (Pearson R = 0.9) to ERCC spike-ins\sjcitep[.]{wuquantitativeassessmentsinglecell2014} Moreover, nanoliter volume reactions used in the Fluidigm C1 consistently identified more genes and had more uniform transcript coverage than microliter volume reactions due to higher enzyme, template and substrate concentrations and lower loss in washes. Continued development led to further sensitivity improvements in Smart-seq3\sjcitep[.]{hagemannjensensinglecellrnacounting2020} CEL-seq, developed in 2012, elected to use linear amplification by in-vitro transcription after reverse transcription rather than PCR-based approaches - effectively translating the 1992 Eberwine protocol to sequencing\sjcitep[.]{hashimshonycelseqsinglecellrnaseq2012} The protocol generated \num{280000} mRNA counts from single \textit{C. elegans} blastomeres. An updated protocol later increased low capture efficiency from \textasciitilde{6}\% to \textasciitilde{22}\% and increased throughput to 96-format using the now trending Fluidigm C1\sjcitep[.]{hashimshony2016celseq2}

In 2013, Sasagawa et al. argued that Smart-seq, CEL-seq and STRT-seq suffered from amplification biases caused by 3' enrichment, template switching, and pseudo-random Tn5 fragmentation, resulting in poor correlation between technical replicates (Pearson R between 0.7 and 0.85)\sjcitep[.]{sasagawa2013quartz} Their method, Quartz-seq, showed several sensitivity and specificity improvements: by employing Exo I digestion after reverse transcription and cDNA Covaris instead of Tn5 fragmentation, the amount of adapter sequences present in the data was reduced from 21\% to 7\%, and technical reproducibility was increased (Pearson R of \textasciitilde{0.93}). Later, Quartz-seq2 would improve throughput by using FAC sorting into 1536-wells, and reduce costs from \$23 to \$0.50 per cell by earlier pooling of the reactions\sjcitep[.]{sasagawa2018quartzseq2} Stringent live-cell selection with FACS, addition of a UMI to the RT primer, optimized poly(dA) tailing of cDNA, and use of Y-shaped Illumina adapters further reduced biases and increased sensitivity, achieving a "UMI conversion rate" of 30-50\%, compared to the original Quartz-seq and Drop-seq's 10\%, and CEL-seq's 22\%\sjcitep[.]{ziegenhainComparativeAnalysis2017, mereu2020benchmarking}

At this point in time, the first plate-based scRNA-seq protocols - particularly Smart-seq - began to spread to other labs, and single-cell sequencing was elected Nature method of the year in 2013\sjcitep[.]{MethodYear20132014} For example, analysis of \num{430} single glioblastoma cells with Smart-seq showed heterogeneous expression of several receptor tyrosine kinases (EGFR, PDGFRA, FGFR1), potentially leading to redundancy and therapeutic resistance\sjcitep[.]{patelSingleCellRNAseq2014} The existence of CD133+, therapy-resistant "glioblastoma stem-like cells" had been shown before\sjcitep[,]{baogliomastemcells2006} but single-cell technologies made deeper characterisation possible. However, due to the high reagent and labour costs per cell (approximately \USD30 per cell plus cost of sequencing), almost all studies from this period were limited to few (hundreds) of cells, mainly sourced from few-cell stage embryonic samples\sjcitep[.]{kolodziejczyktechnologybiologysinglecell2015} Profiling samples with inherently large cell counts and diversity, such as immune systems and neuron clusters, would require a different approach.

In 2014, researchers introduced a strategy to increase the throughput of plate-based methods by reducing costs through early pooling of reactions. In MARS-seq \sjcitep[,]{jaitinMassivelyParallelSingleCell2014}, cells were FAC sorted into 384-well plates and subjected to cell-barcoded reverse transcription. Reactions were then pooled, and second-strand synthesis and subsequent steps occurred in a shared microtube. cDNA was amplified by IVT, fragmented, reverse transcribed, further amplified by PCR, and sequenced. Applied to \textasciitilde{\num{5500}} mouse spleen cells, this approach revealed novel dendritic cell subpopulations with distinct activation of \textit{Irf4}, \textit{TNF}, and \textit{TGF-{\greekfont β}} pathways in response to LPS stimulation.

Several scaling breakthroughs were made in 2015, the "annus mirabilis" for single-cell sequencing methods. First, droplet microfluidics\sjcitep[]{thorsen2001dynamic} was applied to single-cell sequencing. Researchers at David Weitz' lab had been working on using nanoliter droplets to perform antibody screening and rare template PCR\sjcitep[,]{mazutis2013singlecell}  referring to a hypothetical system of encapsulating molecules with a nucleic acid barcode that \textit{"indicates which element is encapsulated in the droplet"}\sjcitep[.]{guoDropletMicrofluidicsHighthroughput2012} This system was realised for single-cell RNA-seq in 2015 by two separate labs, both at Harvard in collaboration with Weitz lab: Macosko and colleagues (McCarroll lab) developed Drop-seq\sjcitep[,]{macoskohighlyparallelgenomewide2015} and Klein and colleagues (Kirschner lab) developed inDrop\sjcitep[.]{kleinDropletBarcodingSinglecell2015,zilionissinglecellbarcodingsequencing2017}

Both methods used droplet microfluidics to encapsulate single-cells in nanoliter volume droplets together with a bead carrying barcoded poly(dT) primers, but their amplification and library preparation strategies differed. Drop-seq used a Smart-seq-like approach: once encapsulated, cells are lysed, and their polyA\textsuperscript{+} mRNA is captured by the beads, which carry a poly(dT) oligonucleotide primer containing a UMI and a cell barcode. The emulsion is then broken, and beads are pooled and washed in saline sodium citrate to minimize cross-bead hybridizations. The beads, still with mRNA hybridized, are resuspended in a reverse transcriptase mix, and RT proceeds on-bead to produce full-length cDNA. After RT, exonuclease I is applied to degrade any unextended primers, reducing noise from unused barcode sequences. The resulting cDNA, which is covalently linked to the bead, is then PCR-amplified. Fragmentation proceeds using Nextera XT Tn5 to generate a sequencing library. All steps involving enzymes happen in a pooled manner, saving reagent cost. Drop-seq's cell purity (absence of cell doublets) was assessed using a mixture of HEK (human) and 3T3 (mouse) cell lines, and ranged between 90.4\% to 98.8\% based on cell concentration used. The method was demonstrated on \num{44808} cells from the P14 mouse retina generated in 7 runs across 4 days, producing 39 cell clusters. \textit{PPP1R17}, a new marker for the poorly-characterized nGnG (non-glycinergic/non-GABAergic) amacrine cells, was identified and validated by histochemistry. Transcript capture efficiency was estimated at \textasciitilde10.7 to 12.8\% using ddPCR validation. Drop-seq marked the first single-cell dataset spanning tens of thousands of cells, and reported a >100-fold reduction in cost and time compared to prior scRNA-seq methods (\textasciitilde6.5 cents per cell, \num{10000} cells in 12 hours). Later, a microfluidics-free version of the protocol, using bead arrays rather than droplets, was developed\sjcitep[.]{gierahnseqwellportablelowcost2017}

inDrop used a different approach to both cell loading and reaction chemistry: cells are encapsulated with soft, deformable hydrogels rather than solid resin beads like Drop-seq, which can be stacked in the microfluidic channel. This ordered stack of beads allows almost every droplet to receive a bead, rather than \textasciitilde{2}\% for the poissonian loading used in Drop-seq\sjcitep[.]{abate2009beating} Reaction chemistry was also different. After encapsulation, UV light releases the barcoded poly(dT) primers from the bead into the droplet, and reverse transcription happens within the droplet. Next, the CEL-seq IVT strategy is used to amplify the barcoded, single-cell transcriptomes in a pooled manner. The protocol was demonstrated on a total of \num{11209} mouse embryonic stem cells, including a time series of leukemia inhibitory factor (LIF) withdrawal-induced differentiation over 7 days. 5 distinct ES sub-populations were identified, including rare lineages marked by epiblast (\textit{Krt8/18}), primitive endoderm (\textit{Col4a1/2}, \textit{Lama1}), and heat shock markers (\textit{Hsp90}, \textit{Hspa5}). Counts agreed strongly with smFISH data (R = 0.92 for mean, 0.90 for CV).

Both Drop-seq and inDrop boasted a significant jump in throughput over pure plate-based sorting methods, from hundreds to several thousands, potentially tens of thousands, of cells per day. Higher cell counts, traded for per-cell sensitivity, now allowed for the profiling of highly varied samples such as immune cells. However, both methods also required fabrication of complex reagents (notably, barcoded beads and microfluidic chips) and complex microfluidic operation. In 2016, the first commercial droplet-microfluidic single-cell sequencing solution was released: 10x Genomics' 3' Single Cell Gene Expession kit and accompanying Chromium microfluidic controller combined barcoded hydrogel beads, enzymes, primers and microfluidics in a relatively simple package. The method drew on concepts from both inDrop and Drop-seq, opting for deformable hydrogel beads for higher cell capture, and a SMART-seq-like, PCR + Tn5 amplification and library preparation strategy. Importantly, the Chromium could load up to 8 samples in parallel, simplifying arrayed experiments. The method was first demonstrated on \num{68000} PBMCs sampled from 8 donors, identifying all major PBMC subtypes (T, NK, B, myeloid) and minor populations (e.g. megakaryocytes)\sjcitep[.]{zhengmassivelyparalleldigital2017} Approximately \num{4500} genes and \num{27000} UMIs were detected on average per cell at a depth of 100k reads/cell, an order of magnitude higher than inDrop and Drop-seq. Mixed-species loading revealied a doublet rate of approximately 3\%.

% 10x's Chromium drew on some technical concepts borrowed from droplet digital PCR (ddPCR), such as emulsion chemistry and microfluidic geometry, which had previously been developed by the company's CSO at Lawrence Livermore\sjcitep[,]{hindsonHighThroughputDropletDigital2011} and commercialized at QuantaLife\sjcitep[.]{brittain2021biorad}

% 2016: SCRB-seq\sjcitep[]{soumillonCharacterizationDirectedDifferentiation2014}
% 2018: mcsCRB-seq\sjcitep[]{bagnoli2018sensitive}

2015's second leap came when researchers in Shendure lab applied combinatorial indexing to single cells in-situ\sjcitep[,]{cusanovich2015multiplex} also marking the first single-cell ATAC sequencing protocol (sci-ATAC-seq, single-cell combinatorial indexing ATAC sequencing). In sci-ATAC, nuclei are tagmented using Tn5 loaded with an array of barcoded adapters in 96-well plates and re-distributed into a second 96-well plate containing PCR mixes with 96 different primer combinations. Each nucleus is thus indexed by a combination of its Tn5 and PCR barcode, and individual cells are never physically separated. The method was applied to \num{14533} cells from 4 different cell lines. Between 50 and 60\% of reads were located in ENCODE-defined DNase I hypersensitivity sites, confirming assay fidelity. Approximately \num{5000} accessible fragments are captured per cell on average. Cross-cell collision rate was estimated at 11\% using species identities in the cell lines, and individual cell barcodes contained \textasciitilde90\% species-pure accessible fragments. Interestingly, 4 different states were observed in GM12878 cells based on accessible enhancers. The same combinatorial indexing strategy was applied to scRNA-seq in 2017, this time profiling \num{49360} \textit{C. elegans} L2 larvae, approximately 50-fold coverage over its 762 somatic cells at this stage\sjcitep[.]{caoComprehensiveSinglecellTranscriptional2017} Instead of $96 \times 96$, split-pooling was performed over $576 \times 960$ possible wells (first during in-situ reverse transcription, then Tn5 fragmentation and PCR). Median UMIs and genes detected in \textit{C. elegans} was 575 UMIs and 431 respectively, identifying 27 major cell types, including rare neurons with only 1 or 2 cells per larva. Reagent costs were estimated at \USD0.03-\USD0.20 per cell. Updated protocols were published in 2022, offering higher sensitivity and lower costs (\USD0.01 per cell)\sjcitep[.]{martinOptimizedSingleNucleus2023} SPLiT-seq (split-pool ligation-based transcriptome sequencing)\sjcitep[,]{rosenberg2018singlecell} an independently developed combinatorial indexing protocol, introduced additional barcode ligation steps to increase barcode complexity to 96\textsuperscript{4}. The method was first applied to generate \num{156049} single-cell transcriptomes from P2 and P11 mouse brains and spinal cords\sjcitep[,]{kuijpers2024split} and was recently commercialized into kits by Parse Biosciences - offering up to 10 million cells indexed per run\sjcitep[.]{parse2025gigalab}

Buenrostro and Greenleaf also released a protocol for ATAC-seq at single-cell level in 2015\sjcitep[.]{buenrostrosinglecellchromatinaccessibility2015} In the protocol, cells are loaded onto 96-plex chips on the Fluidigm C1, and lysed in the nanoliter reaction chamber. Nuclei are then incubated with Tn5 and washed with an EDTA or SDS buffer, releasing Tn5 in-situ. Individual nuclei are PCR-cycled on-chip with barcoded primers, indexing cell identity. Libraries are then collected for further amplification, and sequenced. The C1 scATAC-seq protocol was applied to a consortium of GM12878 (human lymphoblastoid), K562 (human leukemia), H1 hESCs, V6.5 mouse ESCs, EML1 (mouse early hematopoietic), TF1 (human erythroblast), HL-60 (human promyeloblast), and BJ (fibroblast) for a total of \num{1632} cells profiled. On average, \num{73000} fragments aligned to the genome, and around 35\% of reads were located in called peak regions. While the aggregate data was approximately 500-fold more sensitive than bulk ATAC-seq (normalized to cell counts), individual aggregate-accessible peaks contained an average of 0.10 fragments per cell - showing extreme sparsity. Pearson correlation between samples and correlation with ENCODE DNase-seq data was high (R = 0.86 and 0.80).

The sci-ATAC-seq and the Fluidigm C1 protocol require either a custom barcoded Tn5 (not widely available at the time), or an expensive, specialized microfluidics controller. Moreover, the C1 protocol lacked scale, and the sci protocol was low in sensitivity. A relatively simple and robust protocol, leaning on FAC sorting of bulk pre-tagmented nuclei into individual 96/384-well plates, and amplifying their accessible chromatin individually, was published in 2018, providing intermediate scale and sensitivity\sjcitep[.]{chenrapidrobustmethod2018} In 2019, two commercial solutions, both using high-throughput droplet-microfluidics, were released by BioRad and then 10x Genomics. BioRad's product, the ddSEQ SureCell ATAC, was a hard, resin bead-based protocol developed in conjunction with Buenrostro lab\sjcitep[.]{lareaudropletbasedcombinatorialindexing2019} Bulk pre-tagmented nuclei are encapsulated with barcoded hard resin beads on the ddSEQ microfluidic platform. Since super-poissonian loading cannot be achieved without hydrogel beads, only a small percentage of droplets would contain a bead under normal circumstances, resulting in a low cell capture rate of around 2\% - similar to Drop-seq. However, smart application of Tn5 tracking allowed the low capture rate to be circumvented: beads are "overloaded"\llap{,} leading to 2.5 beads rather than < 0.05 encapsulated per droplet on average. Each nucleus' material is thus split over multiple single-cell barcodes. Cell barcodes that are statistically significantly enriched for overlap between their captured fragments can be detected and merged in downstream analysis. The resulting cell capture rate for dscATAC-seq is reported as 95\% (\num{5324} cells recovered from \num{5600} loaded). Genomic alignment and sensitivity were also high, reporting 95\% alignment to reference, and a median of \num{34046} unique reads per cell with \num{58.8}\% in peaks. In addition, barcoded Tn5 ("dsci-ATAC") was demonstrated to further increase throughput to up to \num{100000} cells per device, for a total of \num{510123} single cells profiled in the study. In contrast to the BioRad product, the 10x Genomics solution used hydrogel beads, just like their gene expression kit\sjcitep[.]{satpathyMassivelyParallelSinglecell2019} Bulk pre-tagmented are encapsulated in droplets, where the bead dissolves and releases barcoded primers. In-situ PCR indexes single-nucleus accessible fragments, and a second PCR generates a sequencing-ready library. The method was applied to \num{61806} cells from blood and bone marrow across 16 donors and \num{37818} basal cell carcinoma cells from 7 patients, confirming tumor-specific variation in accessibility and copy numbers. An average of \num{27800} unique fragments were detected per cell, and between 31.5\ and 62.5\% of reads were located within peaks. Based on both articles, it thus appeared that the BioRad's dscATAC was more sensitive than the 10x solution.

A known problem with all classic ATAC-seq methods is that only fragments tagmented with an i5 adapter on one end an i7 adapter on the other are PCR-amplified. Since adapter loading on the Tn5 dimer is a random process, and each insertion site is equally likely to receive either possible combination of i5 and i7 insertions, 50\% of all fragments generated are either i5-i5 or i7-i7 species. Symmetrical strand sci-ATAC (s3-ATAC) solves this problem by introducing a uracil base after Tn5's mosaic end adapters\sjcitep[.]{mulqueenhighcontentsinglecellcombinatorial2021} Uracil-intolerant polymerase is then used in the gap fill step step, generating fragments with incomplete overhang. These overhangs can be reliably targeted with a universal i5 adapter, resulting in fragments which are always i5- and i7-ended - effectively doubling capture.
Application on \num{2175} human and \num{837} mouse brain cells yielded between \num{100280} and \num{178069} median unique reads per cell, approximately 6-fold higher than 10x Genomics scATAC-seq, with between 48.1\% and 78.2\% of reads falling in peaks.

\section{Scaling cell throughput}
The release of the 10x Chromium scRNA-seq kit marked a milestone in single-cell sequencing research. It was user friendly enough, and struck a good balance between cell throughput and single-cell quality. Several atlasing projects were completed using the technology at the hundreds of thousands of cell scale, including human\sjcitep[,]{regev2017human} mouse\sjcitep[,]{tabulamuris2018} and fly\sjcitep[.]{davie2018single} In the latter, \num{157000} high-quality single-cell \textit{D. melanogaster} transcriptomes were generated in 26 runs sampled across 8 timepoints between 0 and 50 days old. CEL-seq2 and SMART-seq2 data supplemented the medium-depth 10x Chromium cells with high-quality "anchors"\llap{.} Novel cell-type-specific gene regulatory networks were inferred from for olfactory projection neurons, and glia using SCENIC. Participant TFs were validated for motif enrichment in these cell types using FACS-assisted ATAC-seq.

Meanwhile, plate-based combinatorial indexing strategies like SPLiT-seq and the sci-family of protocols have in general skirted further the extreme end of the sensitivity/throughput scale, leading to their use in several million-cell scale atlasing projects (\autoref{table:millions}).

Scaling droplet-based methods to million cell numbers is also possible. Cells or nuclei can be distributed over 96-well plates and pre-indexed by reverse transcription with a barcoded primer akin to plate-based combinatorial indexing protocols ("scifi-RNA-seq")\sjcitep[.]{datlingerultrahighthroughput2021} The pre-indexed cells are then pooled and run through 10x Chromium droplet generation together at extremely overloaded concentrations: whereas the 10x Chromium was designed for \textasciitilde\num{10000} cells per channel, scifi-RNA-seq increases this to up to 1.53 million cells per channel without apparent reduction in per-cell data quality. The same principle can be applied to ATAC-seq: scifi-ATAC-seq and txci-ATAC-seq use pre-indexing in 96 wells with barcoded adapter Tn5, enabling the profiling of up to \num{200000} single nuclei per 10x scATAC-seq lane\sjcitep[.]{zhang2024scifi, zhang2024txci} Sensitivity also remained high, sampling between \num{21362} and \num{45622} unique fragments per cell on average across tissues assessed. Interestingly, these findings suggest that the chemical and physical reactions within the nanoliter droplets are not rate-limited by concentration of enzymes and reagents.

Similar approaches have been used not to increase pure throughput, but to scale number of samples per droplet run. Again, the main strategy here is to pre-index or "hash" samples with oligonucleotide barcodes. Hashed samples can then be co-processed through droplet generation together, and the sample hash can computationally separate cells into sample bins post hoc. Hashing can be done by attaching sample-barcoded oligonucleotides to their membranes, either by antibody-antigen binding\sjcitep[.]{stoeckiusCellHashing2018} or lipid/cholesterol embedding\sjcitep[.]{mcginnisMultiSeqSample2019} Within each droplet, the membrane-bound oligonucleotides co-captured together with the associated cell's mRNA or tagmented genome. Antibodies have demonstrated slightly better accuracy, while lipid hashing can be used when high-strength antibodies are not available\sjcitep[.]{mylkacomparativeanalysisantibody2022} The increased precision of antibody-based methods could be attributed to the many successive washes involved, but which may also reduce cell viability and integrity.

Instead of surface antibodies, oligonucleotides can also directly index generic (not custom-barcoded) Tn5. SNuBar (single-nucleotide barcoding) introduces a single oligonucleotide barcode (SBO) during tagmentation\sjcitep[.]{wang2021simple} This SBO contains an 18 bp sample barcode and hybridises to Tn5 transposomes through a universal adapter sequence. During tagmentation these Tn5-SBO complexes remain stable as Tn5 integrates itself into accessible regions. Nuclei are then washed, removing unbound Tn5 and excess SBOs, and samples are pooled. After droplet encapsulation, the SBO is released from Tn5 and captured by the cell barcode during PCR amplification, linking cell barcodes with sample barcodes.

\section{Perturbations}
Large screens that investigate the effect of silencing specific genes using RNA interference emerged in the early 2000s\sjcitep[.]{berns2004large,boutros2006analysis} Such perturbation screens follow either an arrayed design, where perturbations are individually plated and tracked in physical space, or a pooled design, where cell lines are transduced with pooled shRNA vector libraries at a given MOI, and survivors are sequenced. When CRISPR was developed as a molecular tool for rapid and accurate gene editing in 2013\sjcitep[,]{ran2013genome} full genetic knockouts became widely available, producing a pooled genome-wide screen involving \num{64000} sgRNAs for \num{18000} gene knockouts just a year later\sjcitep[.]{shalem2014genome, behan2019prioritization, pacini2021integrated} In addition to genetic knockouts, specific genes can also be up- or downregulated using catalytically dead \textit{Cas9} fused to transcription activators such as \textit{VP64} or \textit{p65}, or repressors such as \textit{KRAB}. Both arrayed and pooled CRISPR/RNAi screens have drawbacks: arrayed screens require vast amounts of labour and reagents, and pooled CRISPR-screens are limited to very simple readouts such as cell survival or marker expression.

In 2016, researchers at the Broad institute devised that CRISPR edits could be performed in a pooled manner with a read-out at the single-cell level - leveraging the newly developed scRNA-seq technologies to also read perturbation identity at the single-cell level\sjcitep[.]{dixit2016perturbseq} In this method, Perturb-seq, each vector is designed to express two transcripts after integrating in the host genome: a first transcript containing the sgRNA, driven by U6, and a second transcript containing a puromycin selection cassette, \textit{BFP}, and a 20 nucleotide long guide barcode (GBC), driven by \textit{EF1{\greekfont α}}. The GBC transcript is polyadenylated, allowing it to be captured and counted through standard 3' scRNA-seq protocols (10x GEX in this specific case). The \{sgRNA:GBC\} dictionary can then be used to identify the genetic edit present in each captured cell\sjcitep[.]{dixit2016perturbseq} This pooled approach allows rapid parallelisation of perturbation screens with limited labour. Using this system, researchers observed that even within the same perturbation, individual cells can exhibit different responses. In particular, knockdown of the key ER chaperone BiP (\textit{HSPA5}) caused two possible responses: some cells preferentially activated \textit{PERK}, while others activated \textit{IRE1{\greekfont α}}. TAP-seq (Targeted Perturb-seq) improves on the original protocol by using targeted gene panel amplification rather than whole-transcriptome analysis\sjcitep[.]{schraivogel2020targeted} 96\% of sequenced reads mapped to target genes compared to \textasciitilde10\% in typical Perturb-seq experiments, achieving the same analytical power as Perturb-seq at 19- to 49-fold lower cost. Application to a d\textit{Cas9-KRAB} vector with sgRNAs targeting \num{1788} candidate enhancers at MOI of 1 identified 81 new enhancer-target gene pairs. These 81 enhancers affected 24\% of the genes in the amplification panel, a hit rate far higher than previous Perturb-seq screens (3.5 - 6.0\%). The assay's costs can be further scaled down by multiplexing perturbations or cells: if gene regulatory networks are assumed sparse in number of participants, then the differential expression of a cell responding to multiple perturbations (high MOI), or a droplet containing multiple single-perturbation cells, can be considered a linear combination of the effect of its components. Pooling cells or perturbations in this manner and demultiplexing the individual terms using PCA can reduce cost ten-fold and quantify interaction terms between different perturbations\sjcitep[.]{yao2024scalable} CROP-seq, a different perturbation platform, uses a poly-adenylated gRNA which is directly captured by 3' scRNA-seq rather than guide barcodes expressed on the selection cassette (\autoref{fig:perturbation_vectors})\sjcitep[.]{datlinger2017pooled} The method was demonstrated using Drop-seq instead of 10x Chromium, showing that the open-source method has sufficient sensitivity to detect the gRNA transcripts. A small screen of Jurkat T cells, knocking out 6 genes and 23 TFs involved in T-cell receptor signaling pathways (87 gRNAs total), confirmed that \textit{LCK}, \textit{ZAP70}, and \textit{LAT} knockouts blocked TCR induction. A total of 5,798 high-quality single-cell transcriptomes were analyzed, with a median of 80 cells per targeted gene.

Perturbation screens have also been coupled with scATAC-seq readouts. In Perturb-ATAC\sjcitep[,]{rubin2019coupled} cells are transduced in pools with Perturb-seq-like vectors. Single cells were then captured and tagmented on the Fluidigm C1, limiting the size of the study to \textasciitilde\num{4300} cells total. The GBCs are reverse-transcribed on-chip, allowing them to be amplified and captured through the ensuing ATAC PCR to generate perturbation-coupled scATAC-seq profiles. Dual knockdown of \textit{EBF1} and \textit{EZH2} (in B lymphoblasts), and \textit{KLF4} and \textit{ZNF750} (in keratinocytes), showed stronger repression than individual perturbations, and this relationship was observed to be cell-type specific. Spear-ATAC is able to overcome Perturb-ATAC's throughput limitations by using droplet encapsulation on 10x Chromium\sjcitep[.]{pierce2021high} After pooled lentiviral transduction, cells undergo the regular 10x scATAC-seq workflow, but with an additional primer added to the droplet encapsulation step. This primer anneals to the integrated lentiviral cassette, amplifying its barcode. By using droplet-based methods, \num{414} sgRNA knockdowns could be profiled across \textasciitilde\num{105000} cells (sampled between 3 and 21 days post transduction). Analyzing the resulting time series revealed time-dependent effects of the knockdowns. For example, \textit{GATA1} knockdown was found to first upregulate \textit{STAT5} motif accessibility, but later \textit{RUNX} motifs. As expected, these GRNs were also observed to be cell-type specific between K562, MCF-7 and GM12878 cells.

% Note: the 2015 buenrostro paper had chemical perturbations. Perturbation of K562 cells with cell-cycle inhibitors showed to reduced variability at cell-cycle-linked regions. JUN inhibition specifically increased JUN-associated region variability, and Imatinib selectively affected G1/S-related sites. In GM12878 cells, TNF-{\greekfont α}{} stimulation elevated variability at NF-\textkappaB target sites.

One disadvantage of the CRISPR screening methods above is that most of the vectors described do not contain \textit{Cas9}, limiting the studies to transgenic and immortal cell lines. Most perturbation studies so far also deal with CRISPRa/i, CRISPR knockouts, but not yet with gene expression constructs (for example: ectopic TF expression). Such studies are considerably more difficult to implement in a pooled manner, since transcript length has an effect on transfection, packaging and transduction efficiency, which in turn biases MOI and dosage. These effects call for the titration of viral concentrations and arrayed screening, which is expensive and laborious.

Single-cell RNA sequencing have recently also been used in chemical screens. For example, application of sci-RNA-seq on \num{5000} wells with 188 different small molecules $\times$ four doses $\times$ duplicates $\times$ 3 cell lines showed that \textit{MEK} inhibitors affect \textit{KRAS}/\textit{ABL}-mutant A549 and K562 cells, but not MCF-7\sjcitep[.]{srivatsan2020massively} 17 different \textit{HDAC} (histone deacetylase) inhibitors each shared a transcriptional trajectory, arresting cell-cycling signaling. Moreover, the large number of cells assessed (\num{649340}) allowed per-condition cell counts to be aggregated into pseudo-viability scores. More recently, an extremely large study applied Parse Biosciences' GigaLab SPLiT-seq to profile 379 drugs across 50 genetically diverse human cancer cell lines, capturing over 95.6 million single-cell transcriptomes (median \num{1287} cells per condition, median \num{1890} transcripts per cell) across \num{52886} unique cell line-drug-dose combinations. All cell lines were grown together to form "mosaic tumor" spheroids in mixed cultures. The screen, a collaboration between Arc institute and Vevo Therapeutics, revealed pronounced transcriptional shifts in response to proteasome, \textit{HDAC}, \textit{PI3K}/\textit{AKT}, and \textit{CDK} inhibitors, and confirmed genotype-specific responses to \textit{RAS}/\textit{RAF} pathway inhibitors like Dabrafenib and RMC-6236.

\section{Single-cell data pre-processing}
\label{sect:computational}
% The newly available human genome, in conjunction with large amounts of data generated by microarray and sequencing-based quantitative assays, necessitated the development of new bioinformatics tools.
Once data is sequenced, and raw \verb|.fastq| data is available, the first step in data analysis is alignment to a reference genome. A number of algorithms exist for this task, and the optimal choice depends on several factors. Short-read aligners such as \verb|bowtie2| are optimized for reads < \SI{200}{\bp}, while long-read aligners like \verb|minimap2| are better at handling reads longer than \SI{1000}{\bp}. The type of sequencing technology used also plays a role: sequencing by synthesis (Illumina) generates more deletion artifacts due to incorporation errors, and these need to be explicitly corrected for by \verb|bwa-mem|, whereas \verb|minimap2| is better suited for handling the higher single-base error rates found in Nanopore/PacBio reads.

Whereas genomic reads, such as generated by ChIP-seq, can be directly aligned to the genome, mRNA transcripts overlap discontinuously with the genome due to splicing, and are thus more challenging to align. Aligning short-read RNA-seq data to the genome requires splice site annotations, which required expressed sequence tags (ESTs) or full-length cDNA sequencing to generate. \verb|TopHat| ended the need for ESTs by introducing computational splice site identification\sjcitep[.]{trapnell2009tophat} The algorithm identifies exons by looking for splice site motifs near the flanks of the reads that only partially map ("initially unmapped") to the reference genome with \verb|bowtie|. Multiple reads are then aggregated to statistically validate a splice site. The resulting read alignments can then be passed to \verb|Cufflinks|\sjcitep[,]{trapnellTranscriptAssemblyQuantification2010} which assembles them into possible transcript isoforms and can provide a statistical estimate of their relative quantities without long-read cDNA sequencing data. Like many classes of algorithms, a trade-off between memory and compute can also be made, for example \verb|STAR| requires large amounts of memory due to its use of an uncompressed genome index, but is faster\sjcitep[,]{dobin2013star} while \verb|HISAT2| is more memory-efficient, but takes longer.

The diversity in sequencing technologies and alignment algorithms led to the use of several independent alignment formats, each tied to specific sequencing technologies or alignment algorithms. Analyses on different datasets (e.g. variant calling, genotype analysis) required cross parsing or conversion. The introduction of the sequence alignment map (\verb|SAM|) format as part of the 1000 Genomes Project in 2009\sjcitep[,]{liSequenceAlignmentMapFormat2009} provided a unified standard for storing aligned sequencing data which has since become the de facto standard. Each read is stored in a single line containing 11 mandatory fields including: read name, reference name (chromosome or contig), position, mapping quality, the actual sequence, several other alignment quality parameters as well as optional tags (\autoref{fig:sam}).

After alignment, the next step is to vectorize the data by counting aligned reads in genomic regions. This generates a cell by feature count matrix, unlocking linear algebra (\autoref{tab:feature_count_matrix}). The region sets used for counting differ by assay used. scRNA-seq requires gene annotations, whereas for scATAC-seq several strategies exist: ideally, previously validated sample-specific peak regions are used to limit data sparsity. These peak sets can be called from previous local data with peak callers like \verb|MACS|\sjcitep[.]{zhang2008macs} \verb|MACS| uses a modeling-based approach: reads are shifted by half the learned fragment length to center to true binding sites (ChIP-seq bias). A sliding window scans the genome and uses a Poisson background (scaled to control when available) to call regions whose enrichment is statistically significant. It also removes PCR duplicates beyond what random sampling predicts. \verb|MACS| outperformed contemporary tools (\verb|ChIPSeq Peak Finder|, \verb|FindPeaks|, \verb|QuEST|) by calling more true peaks, showing higher motif enrichment and better summit-to-motif resolution while keeping lower false positives.  MACS2 is a faster, multithreaded version with better QC metrics and broader assay support (ChIP-seq, ATAC-seq, DNase-seq) compatible with paired-end reads\sjcitep[.]{gaspar2020macs2} When sample-specific peak sets are not available, public data or genomic bins can be used. Particularly, the ENCODE SCREEN regions is a set of \num{926535} human and \num{339815} mouse candidate cis-regulatory elements (cCREs) identified based on ATAC-seq, DNase hypersensitivity, ChIP-seq, histone modifications (H3K4me3, H3K27ac), and CTCF binding aggregated from over \num{9000} efforts\sjcitep[.]{encode2020expanded} These regions represent potential enhancers, promoters, and insulators. Finally, the selected region set should be filtered for problematic regions to remove false peaks\sjcitep[.]{amemiya2019encode}

Raw count matrices contain all counts in all barcodes sequenced in the dataset. Filtering true cells from background noise barcodes is a dataset-defining step for any single-cell dataset. The barcode-rank plot (also referred to as "knee" or "elbow plot") visualizes the distribution of reads across all sequenced barcodes in the sequencing dataset (\autoref{fig:kneeplot}). It is obtained by counting the number of reads per barcode, and ranking each barcode in a log-log figure - similarly in concept to a Zipf or Lorenz curve.

	\label{fig:kneeplot}
\end{figure}

In the "ideal signal" case, all reads are evenly distributed across a small number of barcodes that matches the number of input cells (e.g. \num{10000} in the figure). This means that there are no quality differences between real cells, and there are no barcodes with low read counts. The opposite scenario ("ideal noise") has an even distribution of all reads across \textit{all} barcodes, and we cannot distinguish true cell barcodes from noise barcodes. Real datasets fall between these two extremes. A high quality dataset exhibits a clear cut-off, where the number of reads per barcode sharply drops off in the transition between real cells and background noise. A dataset of lower quality will exhibit a more gradual transition between real cells and background noise. Based on this simple plot, we can estimate at a glance the rough quality characteristics of any single-cell dataset:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
	\item The number of cells captured in the dataset is given by the barcode rank where true cells are separated from background noise ("knee"). If the dataset is sequenced to near-saturation, the total number of barcodes can even exceed the whitelist, as sequencing/PCR/synthesis errors introduce non-whitelisted species.
	\item The approximate number of reads associated with each true cell can be estimated from the y-axis.
	\item Overall data quality can be estimated by comparing the area under real cells and area under noise, or simply the "sharpness" of the cutoff.
	\item Total noise can be inferred by looking at the number of reads present after the true cell cutoff, taking into account that an equal fraction of noise is also present in the true cells, for example stemming from cross-contamination or bead impurities\sjcitep[.]{lareau2020inference}
\end{itemize}

\section{Single-cell data post-processing}
% [It would actually be better to structure this paragraph as such: "compared to rna-seq data, scrna-seq data has two problems: one it is more non-linear because of the added cell states and two drop-outs additionally make it more difficult to do statistical test. this is solved by...:"]

Conventional bulk RNA-seq analysis tools modeled gene counts using Poissonian (\verb|edgeR|)\sjcitep[]{robinsonEdgeRBioconductorPackage2010} or negative binomial (\verb|DESeq|)\sjcitep[]{andersDifferentialExpressionAnalysis2010} distributions. Statistical tests can then be used to identify statistically significant differentially expressed genes. Directly visualising correlations or variance between many RNA-seq or microarray samples is difficult due to the high-dimensionality of the data. Traditionally, this was done by first reducing the dimensionality of the data using PCA, and then visualising the first 2 principal components in a 2D space. Alternatively, heatmaps with dendrograms were used to order correlated samples.

Compared to bulk RNA-seq and microarray data, single-cell sequencing data is highly zero-inflated: when sampling transcripts in a single cell, the probability of weakly-expressed genes not being detected is high, leading to large amounts of "dropouts" (false negative gene expression). This sparsity, couplied with the more fine-grained nature of cell states in single-cell data further introduces non-linearity, since gradients of expression in the population are no longer averaged out. This problem was partially solved by the introduction of \verb|t-SNE|, a technique first designed to visualize the MNIST dataset of handwritten digits\sjcitep[.]{maatenVisualizingHighDimensionalData2008} The earliest implementations of \verb|t-SNE| were used for single-cell mass cytometry data (45 protein levels per cell)\sjcitep[,]{amirViSNEEnablesVisualization2013} then single-cell qPCR (175 genes)\sjcitep[,]{saadatpourCharacterizingHeterogeneityLeukemic2014} and later single-cell whole transcriptomics(after PCA on \textasciitilde{\num{20000}} genes)\sjcitep[.]{macoskohighlyparallelgenomewide2015} \verb|t-SNE| preserves local structure (close neighborhood of cells) at the cost of some concessions to global structure: clusters of single cells can appear closer in the \verb|t-SNE| embedding than they are in high-dimensional gene space. \verb|UMAP| is a slightly newer dimensionality reduction algorithm which aims to better preserve global structure\sjcitep[.]{bechtDimensionalityReductionVisualizing2018} Some common criticisms of both \verb|t-SNE| and \verb|UMAP| is that they still distort global topologies\sjcitep[.]{chariSpeciousArtSinglecell2023} In addition, both methods are sensitive to hyperparameters (perplexity, number of neighbours) and stochasticity (e.g. initialisation). These tools are thus only used for exploration and quick visualisation of the data.

% \verb|SCDE| corrects for dropouts by taking into account aggregate expression levels across all cells to calculate dropout probabilities\sjcitep[.]{kharchenkoBayesianApproachSinglecell2014} These dropout probabilities are then used as priors to model gene expression levels in individual cells. Dropouts are weighted based on their prior probability, and likely relative (fold-change) gene expression levels are inferred. This approach ensures that highly-expressed genes are not penalized, and low-expressed genes are not inflated.

% Monocle\sjcitep[]{trapnellDynamicsRegulatorsFate2014} Advent of single-cell RNA-sequencing --> we can now look at gene expression dynamics (not possible in bulk rna-seq due to simpson's paradox). Differentiate human myoblasts to [skeletal muscle], sample 270 cells over 4 timepoints between 0 and 72 hours using the Fluidigm C1, 4M reads/cell, compare also with bulk rna-seq of each timepoint. Then Monocle: TopHat and CuffLinks are used to generate a count matrix, independent component analysis is used for dimreduc (why?). finally, a minimum-spanning tree (graph that connects cells with minimal total edge length, with edges representing transcriptional similarity), then monocle identifies the longest path along the MST. a starting cell (highly proliferative progenitor) is chosen and cells are ordered along the path. branch points may be detected. expression profiles are also smoothed along the trajectory using generalized additive models. now genes can be correlated to pseudotime.

% Simpson's paradox prohibits accurate investigation of gene expression dynamics in bulk samples. Single-cell datasets, however, can be both larger and much more fine-grained than bulk RNA-seq datasets sampled across different conditions or timepoints. \verb|Monocle| leverages this property inferring differentiation state from single-cell sequencing data sampled from differentiating cells\sjcitep[.]{trapnellDynamicsRegulatorsFate2014} High-dimensional gene expression space is reduced to statistically significant dimensions, and a minimum spanning tree is calculated between cells in this space. The longest path within this tree can then be used to calculate a "pseudotime" property. Beyond being able to order cells according to their differentiation state, genes can also be correlated with pseudotime to identify significant differentiation drivers.

By 2019, several single-cell sequencing technologies had emerged. Each had their own strengths and weaknesses, sometimes leading to technical artifacts in datasets even when assessing the same biological sample. Even the same technology used on different days could lead to batch effects due to sample degradation or other technical artifacts between experiments. This translates to cells clustering by dataset batch rather than biological state. \verb|Seurat| aims to facilitate integration of multiple single-cell datasets across different technologies and conditions\sjcitep[.]{stuartcomprehensiveintegrationsinglecell2019} The package uses canonical correlation analysis (CCA)\sjcitep[]{hotellingRelationsTwoSets1936} to calculate linear combinations of genes (canonical correlation vectors, in this case "metagenes") that capture common information between two datasets. Cells are then co-projected in this shared metagene space to create a new "joint embedding" where clustering and differential expression analysis can be performed. The canonical vectors can be used to re-transform differential metagenes back to the original gene set used. \verb|Seurat|'s CCA implementation could also be used for label transfer: if one dataset is labeled or annotated, proximity in the joint embedding space can be used to transfer labels from one dataset to another. Similarly to \verb|Seurat|, \verb|Scanpy| unifies preprocessing, highly variable gene selection, PCA, graph-based clustering, \verb|t-SNE|/\verb|UMAP| embedding, differential expression in a python package\sjcitep[.]{wolf2018scanpy} The cells by gene count matrix is stored along unstructured metadata in the \verb|AnnData| format.

Whereas scRNA-seq is limited to counts in \textasciitilde{\num{22000}} genes (human, mouse), the accessible genome can span hundreds of thousands of open chromatin regions which can each be sampled a maximum of two times per cell. These effects lead to highly-dimensional, but sparse count matrices. Latent semantic indexing (LSI), an approach borrowed from text analysis, can be used to perform cell clustering and dimensionality reduction\sjcitep[.]{cusanovichsinglecellatlasvivo2018} Briefly, single-cell accessibility is first counted in either annotated regions or 5 kb genome bins, binarized, and scaled according to inverse document frequency (peaks that are not commonly accessible are weighted higher). Singular value decomposition (SVD), is then used to decompose the resulting weighted cell-accessibility matrix into latent features across cells:

		\end{bmatrix}}
\]

Here, $m$ is the number of peaks, $n$ is the number of cells, and $r = \min(m,n)$ is the rank of the decomposition. U represents the peak-composition of the latent features, $\Sigma$ is diagonal and scales each latent feature, and V\textsuperscript{T} represents the contribution of each latent feature to each cell. Based on $\Sigma$, we can select the top \verb|n| most important features and cluster cells in this reduced space using \verb|t-SNE|. Differentially accessible regions (DARs) between clusters can then be calculated on the raw binary accessibility matrix using logistic regression, which is suited to model the odds of a binary event like chromatin accessibility.

Accessibility within each cistrome is normalized and converted to z-scores, which are then used for \verb|t-SNE| and clustering. Instead of known TF motifs, k-mers can also be used to identify novel motifs important in a given biological process.

	\cdot
	\underset{k \times n}{

LDA parameters (probabilities) are estimated using a Markov chain monte carlo approach: regions are initially randomly assigned to topics, and topic-region assignment is then updated iteratively in each cell based on two factors: (1) the topic's occurrence in the cell and (2) the region's occurrence in each topic across all other cells. Sampling is only performed in this latent topic-assignment vector for each region in each cell (collapsed Gibbs sampling), which reduces total computational complexity compared to sampling in both region-topic and topic-cell distribution. This approach is more efficient than other Bayesian approaches (e.g. variational inference) due to the extreme sparsity of the data. \verb|t-SNE| or \verb|UMAP| can then be used to visualize the resulting normalized topic-cell distributions. LDA's topics are also interpretable\sjcitep[,]{steyversProbabilisticTopicModels2007} a property that is exploited to extract transcription factor binding motifs from the cis-regulatory topics found. Motif enrichment analyses can be performed using \verb|cisTarget|\sjcitep[.]{imrichova2015icisTarget} Regions and combinations of regions can also be annotated using nearest genes using \verb|ChIPseeker|\sjcitep[.]{yu2015chipseeker}

\verb|cisTopic| also introduces a region score which rewards regions that are more specific to a topic, and penalises regions that are ubiquitous across topics:

\[
	\text{Region score} = \beta_{r,t} \left( \log \beta_{r,t} - \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \log \beta_{r,t} \right)
\]

	Then the term: \[\beta_{r,t} \log \beta_{r,t}\] can be considered the local information content of the region in topic context, while:

The region score is thus an average-normalized measure of a given region's information content, indicating how specific it is to each topic. Topics can then be scored for TF motif enrichment by counting TF motif occurrence in the score-ranked regions and calculating the area under this rank curve (\verb|AUCell|)\sjcitep[.]{aibarscenicsinglecell2017} \verb|cisTopic|'s LDA approach was benchmarked against other popular tools such as \verb|chromVAR|, \verb|scABC|, \verb|BROCKMAN|, \verb|SCRAT|, \verb|Cicero|, and LSI, using both simulated single-cell ATAC-seq datasets and real data from FAC-sorted hematopoietic cells. \verb|cisTopic| typically yielded higher scores for cell-type clustering, especially in low-coverage scenarios, confirming that the method can more robustly capture biologically meaningful patterns in sparse single-cell ATAC-seq data.

\section{Deep-learning models}
\label{sect:deep-learning-models}
Recently, the availability of massive public databases and availability of compute resources have led to widespread application of deep learning in biology. Advances in algorithms trace back to 1959, when Hubel and Wiesel showed that specific neurons in a cat's visual cortex respond to common spatial patterns such as edges, corners, and orientations, in specific locations within the cat's visual field\sjcitep[.]{hubel1959receptive} These patterns can overlap when layers of neurons pass information onwards, forming broader structures which ultimately allow the cat to recognise complex objects. This idea was borrowed to implement convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for handwritten digit recognition\sjcitep[.]{fukushima1980neocognitron,lecun1998gradient} Rasterized (vectorized) images are scanned by a "filter"\llap{,} converting the original data into a new representation. This new representation is then passed on to the next layer, where it is again scanned by a new filter, and so on until the final layer. CNNs are thus well suited for detecting spatial patterns, and integrating these patterns into larger, more global structures. as a result, CNNs have been successfully used in enhancer prediction models. An early example was DeepSEA\sjcitep[,]{zhou2015predicting} a CNN trained on ENCODE and Roadmap Epigenomics data (690 TF ChIP-seq + 125 DHS + 104 H3K27ac/H3K4Me3 datasets). This model could simultaneously predict these 919 chromatin features at 1 base resolution with higher accuracy than previous tools (\verb|CADD|, \verb|GWAVA|). Saturation mutagenesis analyses showed that DeepSEA could de novo predict known allelic imbalances and eQTLs (using chromatin features as a proxy).

Several track-predicting foundation models have also been developed. In 2018 Basenji2 was published, a CNN trained on \num{4229} human epigenomic (DNase-seq, histone-ChIP) and transcriptional (CAGE) tracks across more than 500 cell and tissue types. With a large receptive field of 131 kb\sjcitep[,]{kelley2018sequential} the model could predict 128-bp binned coverage across the genome: users feed any 131-kb reference or mutant sequence to the pretrained model (or fine-tune on new tracks) and obtain coverage predictions for each of the \num{4229} tissue-specific tracks it was trained on. In 2021, Enformer replaced dilated convolutions with global self-attention and pushed the receptive field to 100 kb with a 200 kb input window\sjcitep[.]{avsec2021effective} Trained now on \num{5313} human and \num{1643} mouse tracks (TF and histone-mark ChIP, DNase I, ATAC and CAGE), it predicts these tracks at 128 bp resolution, including long-range interactions. Borzoi is another CNN-attention hybrid, trained on thousands of CAGE, DNase, ATAC, and ChIP-seq datasets similarly to Basenji2 and Enformer, but also adding 866 human and 279 mouse RNA-seq tracks\sjcitep[.]{linder2025predicting} The model predicts these tracks now at 32 bp-resolution. Kundaje lab introduced ChromBPNet, a CNN trained on ENCODE ATAC-seq and DNase-seq tracks for the five tier 1 ENCODE cell lines (GM12878, HepG2, K562, IMR90, H1-hESC)\sjcitep[.]{pampari2025} A smaller sub-net learned each enzyme's sequence biases based on non-peak regions signal, allowing ChromBPNet to isolate each motif's true footprint independent from assay used. This allows the model to predict, at single base resolution, the accessibility read profile (shape + total counts) that either ATAC-seq or DNase-seq would produce.

Recent work has also shown that single-cell datasets can be mined directly to learn enhancer grammar and design new regulatory elements. scRNA-seq of nine patient-derived cultures uncovered an intermediate state that co-expresses melanocytic factors (\textit{SOX10}, \textit{MITF}) and mesenchymal regulators (\textit{SOX6}, \textit{EGR3})\sjcitep[.]{wouters2020robust} Follow-up bulk ATAC-seq at four time points after \textit{SOX10} knock-down showed gradual chromatin shifts rather than abrupt switches. Expanding to 26 melanoma samples from six species, Minnoye et al. found SOX10/MITF/TFAP2A-rich enhancers in melanocytic cells and AP-1/TEAD motifs in mesenchymal cells, with motif usage conserved in dog, horse, pig and zebrafish\sjcitep[.]{minnoye2020cross} The DeepMEL model, CNN-RNN trained on \num{339099} human ATAC peaks, predicted state-specific enhancer activity from sequence alone and guided the synthesis of new MES and MEL enhancers. A similar approach has been used to decode \textit{Drosophila} brain development. Janssens and colleagues profiled \num{240919} scATAC-seq nuclei from the developing \textit{Drosophila} brain across nine time points\sjcitep[.]{janssens2022decoding} Peaks were clustered with cisTopic to obtain topic sets of co-accessible, cell-type-specific regions. DeepFlyBrain was trained to predict each topic's aggregated accessibility from 2 kb of sequence. SHAP DeepExplainer scores highlighted the nucleotides most responsible for each prediction, revealing, for example, that \textit{eyeless} and \textit{mef2} motifs act synergistically when placed in close proximity, whereas repressor motifs can override activators. In-silico saturation mutagenesis confirmed the minimal length of active enhancers (as short as 49 bp), and reporter studies showed that 41 of 63 synthesized candidates drove the expected cell-type expression in transgenic flies. Building on these workflows, the \verb|CREsted| pipeline trains compact CNNs (e.g. DeepBICCN2, 6M parameters) directly on scATAC-seq peaks, interprets motif logic and performs de novo enhancer design. Starting from topic-assigned peaks or pseudobulk heights, models are first pretrained on the full peak set and then fine-tuned for cell-type specificity. In mouse motor cortex, \verb|CREsted| reached Pearson r \textasciitilde 0.82 on held-out peaks and retrieved the motifs underlying 171 in-vivo-validated enhancers. A human PBMC version recovered every known TF footprint at the CD79A and TCR{\greekfont α}{} enhancers and captured 34\% more informative bases than Borzoi. DeepZebrafish, trained on \num{793273} peaks across \num{639} zebrafish cell-type/time-point combinations, served as an oracle for greedy in-silico evolution: five of seven 2 kb designs drove reporter expression specifically in cardiac muscle, somatic muscle or endothelial cells at 48 hours post fertilisation. Domain or sample-focused models like DeepMEL, DeepFlyBrain, DeepZebraFish complement large foundation models such as Basenji2, Enformer and scFoundation: they require far less compute, yet excel at highly specific questions like cell-type enhancer logic.

Introduced in 2017, the transformer model architecture abandoned recurrence and convolutions in favour of multi-head self-attention blocks\sjcitep[.]{vaswani2023attentionneed} Every token attends to every other, so gradients propagate more directly and training parallelises across all sequence positions. This paper triggered the field-wide shift to attention-only networks that now dominate NLP, vision and sequence biology. For example, meta's Evolutionary Scale Modeling (ESM) applies transformer architecture (specifically, BERT) to protein language to predict masked tokens in amino acid sequences. The log-likelihood of proposed sequences serves as an estimate for how disruptive given mutations will be, allowing researchers to triage synthetic designs. ESM-3 also introduced multimodal masked token modeling: joint tokenized amino-acid sequence, atomic structure, and region annotations can be passed partially masked, and the model will jointly reconstruct missing tokens. This led to the generation of esmGFP, a completely synthetically designed fluorescent protein.

In a similar vein, the original DNABERT was an 86M parameter transformer trained on the 3.2 Gbp human reference genome to classify promoters, TF-binding sites, and other regulatory features\sjcitep[.]{ji2021dnabert} The model was competitive with ClinVar for SNV scoring, but had a relatively small context window of 512 bp, so could not compete in long range tasks such as enhancer-promoter pairing. Nucleotide Transformer (NT) extended the BERT approach to multi-species data: adding both genomic and evolutionary variety by using \num{3300} individual human genomes and 850 other organisms, totaling 262B tokens. NT consistently outperformed BERT and DeepSEA on promoter, enhancer, chromatin, and histone mark prediction, and enabled zero-shot scoring of SNPs and indels - achieving better accuracy than previous models on ClinVar benchmarks\sjcitep[.]{dallatorre2025nucleotide} Recently, Evolutionary Scale Modeling for Genomes (Evo 2) pushed this scaling further with a 40B-parameter model trained on 9.3T tokens spanning all domains of life\sjcitep[.]{brixi2025genome} The model scores variant effects across taxa, and supports up to 1 million tokens of context, enabling the generation of entire synthetic mitochondrial or bacterial genomes. Loss decreased smoothly with more data and parameters, with no saturation observed so far.

AlphaFold 1 was a CNN trained on protein structures (PDB) and multiple sequence alignments (MSAs) to predict residue-residue distances\sjcitep[.]{senior2020improved} AlphaFold 2 switched architecture, passing MSAs through Evoformer, a model with transformer-based self-attention, to predict spatial contacts and ultimately complete 3D protein structure\sjcitep[.]{jumper2021highly} These changes led it to achieve a median backbone accuracy of \SI{0.96}{\angstrom} in CASP14 - surpassing the next best method by \textasciitilde\SI{2}{\angstrom}. AlphaFold 3 included a generative diffusion model to handle atomic coordinates directly rather than relying on MSAs. As a result, the model is capable of predicting binding interactions across protein, DNA/RNA, and small molecules, and performs better than AlphaFold 2 on other tasks.

Geneformer applies the transformer playbook to a collection of 30M human scRNA-seq cells across various tissues, with cells tokenized as a ranked list of their top 2048 genes sorted by expression (no count information)\sjcitep[.]{theodoris2023transferlearning} A transformer architecture is then trained to fill in masked gene positions, implicitly learning patterns of co-expression and regulation across tissues and conditions present in the data. The model is designed to fine-tuned for specific tasks such as dosage-sensitivity classification, regulatory range prediction, and chromatin-state inference. scFoundation, another masked-prediction transformer, was trained on 100M single cells\sjcitep[.]{hao2024large} Varying size from 3M to 100M parameters showed that its loss curves followed the same token vs FLOPs power laws seen in language models.

\section{Motivation and approach}
At the start of our project in 2018, single-cell sequencing methods had become established research tools, but were still expensive and complicated enough to preclude routine use. At the time, academic scRNA-seq protocols were either low in throughput\sjcitep[,]{islamCharacterizationSinglecellTranscriptional2011, ramskoldFulllengthMRNASeqSinglecell2012, hashimshonycelseqsinglecellrnaseq2012, sasagawa2013quartz} or low in sensitivity\sjcitep[,]{macoskohighlyparallelgenomewide2015, gierahnseqwellportablelowcost2017, caoComprehensiveSinglecellTranscriptional2017,kleinDropletBarcodingSinglecell2015} and reserved for use by specialised labs (see \autoref{sect:single_cell_sequencing_tech}). The majority of cells were sequenced using the 10x Chromium, a commercial device/kit which packaged inDrop's droplet microfluidic cell isolation strategy with aspects of Drop-seq and SMART-seq's RNA library preparation into a user-friendly package\sjcitep[.]{zhengmassivelyparalleldigital2017} The Chromium provided a convenient - and more importantly \textit{reliable} - way to generate high-quality single-cell RNA-seq libraries\sjcitep[]{mereuBenchmarkingSinglecellRNAsequencing2020} and struck a comfortable balance between throughput and sensitivity.

We made three observations:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
	\item At a cost of ~\euro{\num{10000}} per 8 samples of 10 000 cells, the 10x scRNA kit was ill-suited for exploratory research.
	\item The kit operated as a black box, with limited possibility for modification.
	\item The kit's sensitivity was around 10 times higher than inDrop and Drop-seq's, implying that the sensitivity ceiling for droplet-based methods had not yet been reached by open-source methods.
\end{enumerate}

In addition, a droplet-based single-cell ATAC sequencing method was not available yet, again constraining researchers to either low-sensitivity\sjcitep[]{cusanovichMultiplexSinglecellProfiling2015} or low-throughput\sjcitep[]{buenrostrosinglecellchromatinaccessibility2015,chenrapidrobustmethod2018} methods. Since droplet methods had provided a balanced trade-off between throughput and sensitivity that fit our lab's scRNA-seq needs\sjcitep[,]{davie2018single} we set out to develop an equivalent for scATAC-seq. We also understood that combined scRNA/ATAC-seq would be necessary to deconvolve the individual and (cross-) combined effects of gene expression and chromatin accessibility in future experiments. A better understanding of how to design and build a droplet-based scATAC-seq method would be a good stepping stone to building multiomics technologies. Specifically, we wanted to build an open-source droplet platform capable of both scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq, at higher sensitivities than inDrop and Drop-seq. This work had already been initiated before my arrival at the lab, and I spent my first year as a master thesis student contributing to this project. Further researching and developing this method, which we later named HyDrop, occupied the first half of this PhD project.

Throughout the development of HyDrop-ATAC, we were often confronted with questions surrounding the method's sensitivity (the number of fragments detected per cell) and specificity (the enrichment of these fragments in enhancers and near transcription start sites, collectively forming chromatin accessibility "peaks"). Such questions arose both internally and externally, and echoed previous calls for an in-depth method performance exploration since the release of Bio-Rad\supershortnotecite{lareaudropletbasedcombinatorialindexing2019} and 10x Genomics'\supershortnotecite{satpathyMassivelyParallelSinglecell2019} scATAC-seq products in 2019. At the time, a number of open-source scATAC-seq protocols existed\sjcitep[,]{cusanovichsinglecellatlasvivo2018,chenrapidrobustmethod2018} but required significant levels of expertise (e.g. automated liquid handling for scaling) or non-commercially available components (e.g. indexed Tn5). Both BioRad and 10x's scATAC-seq solutions were more user-friendly and handled the most technically challenging aspect, microfluidic droplet generation, on benchtop devices (10x's Chromium Controller and Bio-Rad's ddSEQ). However, the Chromium controller already enjoyed a wide presence in large research centres due to the success of 10x's scRNA-seq products, and the Fluidigm C1 had fallen out of use due to throughput and per-cell price considerations. As a result, 10x dominated the commercial scATAC-seq market from 2016 onward, leading to \textasciitilde{1000} publications compared to ddSEQ's \textasciitilde{70} by 2025.

We reasoned that an exact quantification of the performance differences among commercial and non-commercial protocols could stimulate informed decision making. Several such benchmarks had been performed for scRNA-seq methods: the earliest by Wu and Quake\sjcitep[,]{wu2013quantitative} one by Ziegenhain and Enard\sjcitep[,]{ziegenhainComparativeAnalysisSingleCell2017} and a larger, more recent study by Mereu and Heyn\sjcitep[.]{mereuBenchmarkingSinglecellRNAsequencing2020} In the Mereu benchmark, a reference sample was distributed to collaborating labs, which then each generated at least one scRNA-seq dataset. This reference consisted of a mixture of PBMCs, mouse colon cells, HEK293T, 3T3 and dog MDCK cells. This approach complicated genome alignment and further post-processing, and we therefore opted to limit the reference sample to a mixture of human PBMC extracted from one male and one female donor. The use of only liquid tissue also simplified sample handling: PBMC can be distributed frozen and thawed slowly before nuclei extraction. Finally, a large reference dataset of single-cell PBMC chromatin accessibility did not yet exist, ensuring that our benchmark's final dataset could also provide value as a public resource.
