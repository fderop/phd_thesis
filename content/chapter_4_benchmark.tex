\chapter{Benchmarking the performance of single-cell chromatin accessibility sequencing methods}
\setcounter{figure}{1}  % Set the figure counter to 1 already to include the first figure
The following chapter will describe how we benchmarked several of the most prominent scATAC-seq techniques available in 2023. We performed 47 experiments across 8 different scATAC-seq methods to generate 169k single-cell chromatin accessibility profiles. Our benchmark quantified reproducible technical biases in not only the sensitivity, but also the specificity of these methods, produced a scATAC-seq pre-processing pipeline with best practices tutorials, and yielded the largest dataset of scATAC-seq in PBMCs to date. This work was published in \fullcite{deropSystematicBenchmarkingSinglecell2023} (\autoref{fig:article_title_benchmark}).

\section{Motivation and approach}
Throughout the development of HyDrop-ATAC, we were often confronted with questions surrounding the method's sensitivity (the number of fragments detected per cell) and specificity (the enrichment of these fragments in enhancers and near transcription start sites, collectively forming chromatin accessibility "peaks"). Such questions arose both internally and externally, and echoed previous calls for an in-depth method performance exploration since the release of Bio-Rad\supershortnotecite{lareauDropletbasedCombinatorialIndexing2019} and 10x Genomics'\supershortnotecite{satpathyMassivelyParallelSinglecell2019} scATAC-seq products in 2019. At the time, a number of open-source scATAC-seq protocols had also been published\sjcitep[,]{chenRapidRobustMethod2018,mulqueenHighcontentSinglecellCombinatorial2021} [sci, name each of these techniques individually with ref, also mulqueen was not published yet!], but best-case data from these articles revealed that they could not compete with 10x in terms of sensitivity. Furthermore, some of these protocols required significant levels of expertise (e.g. low-level automated liquid handling) and/or non-commercially available components (e.g. indexed Tn5), and were thus exclusive to a handful of labs (see [ref to introduction] for an in-depth review of these methods). Both BioRad and 10x's scATAC-seq solutions were more user-friendly and ran on benchtop microfluidic devices (10x's Chromium Controller and Bio-Rad's ddSEQ), but the Chromium controller was already ubiquitously present in our research due to the success of 10x's scRNA-seq products. As a result, 10x enjoyed a relatively competition-free dominance of the scATAC-seq market, leading to [x] publications compared to ddSEQ's [y], and only approximately [z] publications generated with open-source methods by [date].

We reasoned that an exact quantification of the performance distance between and among commercial and non-commercial protocols could stimulate informed decision making, and possibly reduce market frictions. Two large such studies had already been performed for scRNA-seq methods: one by Christoph Ziegenhain and Wolfgang Enard\sjcitep[,]{ziegenhainComparativeAnalysisSingleCell2017} and a larger, more recent study by Elisabetta Mereu and Holger Heyn\sjcitep[.]{mereuBenchmarkingSinglecellRNAsequencing2020} Both of these benchmarks employed the same strategy: a reference sample was compiled centrally and distributed to collaborating labs [doublecheck for Ziegenhain], which were each tasked with generating at least one scRNA-seq dataset on this sample. In the Mereu benchmark, the reference consisted of a carefully ratioed mixture of [x] tissues across [y] species, which complicated genome alignment and further post-processing during the analysis stage. We therefore opted to limit the reference sample to PBMC extracted from one male and one female donor. The use of a liquid tissue also simplified the sample extraction procedure: frozen PBMC only need to be thawed slowly, after which nuclei extraction can occur immediately on the resulting single-cell suspension. In addition, a large reference dataset of single-cell PBMC chromatin accessibility did not yet exist, and could be of considerable value to blood (cancer) and immune research.

We included 7 different techniques in the initial phase of our benchmark:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item 10x Genomics scATAC-seq v1 and v1.1: The most widely used scATAC-seq method. While v1 reagent kits were technically still available for purchase, the microfluidic chip required for this kit had been decomissioned due to patent infringement litigation by BioRad [doublecheck this. was the v1 hard-linked to the v1 chip? could it be run on nextgem?]. An updated version (v1.1) was released together with the nextGEM chip, which circumvented the BioRad intellectual property by generating droplets in a reservoir deposition style instead of using flow focusing (fig?).
    \item 10x Multiome [check full name]: Introduced in [year], the 10x multiome kit generated both scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq data from the same cells. This novelty had previously only been demonstrated in plates [ref, doublecheck - when was scCAT-seq?]
    \item Bio-Rad ddSEQ SureCell ATAC: 10x's direct competitor. The Bio-Rad scATAC-seq method also used droplet microfluidics, but employed hard resin beads (like Drop-seq)\supershortnotecite{macoskoHighlyParallelGenomewide2015a} and a ingenious overloading strategy to reach cell capture rates on par with 10x [ref introduction ddSEQ].
    \item mtscATAC-seq: A modification on the 10x protocol where omission of certain detergents during nuclei extraction keeps the cell membrane remain intact but permeabilized to Tn5. As a result, the cell's mitochondria are retained, and their genomes is also sequenced, allowing lineage tracing.
    \item s3-ATAC: The most sensitive combinatorial indexing/plate-based assay to date. s3-ATAC uses uracil-intolerant polymerases to terminate extension on the [x] chain. , circumventing the traditional 50\% loss in sensitivity due to homotypic-ended fragments [is there a ref that describes this problem?].
    \item HyDrop: Our own in-house method. HyDrop's workflow is very similar to 10x scATAC-seq, but uses commercially available reagents and the Onyx microfluidics controller in combination with an open-source chip to encapsulate cells.
\end{itemize}

Several other techniques were considered, but not included for various reasons:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Fluidigm C1 scATAC-seq: This was the first commercially available scATAC-seq method.
    \item Chen \& Teichmann protocol: The first open-source scATAC-seq method. This method used up-front bulk tagmentation followed by FAC sorting into individual wells where accessible chromatin fragments from each cell were amplified separately [check this]
    \item sci-ATAC: Plans were made to include sci-ATAC, but due to practical reasons and limited labour bandwidth at competent labs, these were aborted. We sought to represent combinatorial indexing methods with s3-ATAC instead.
    \item dscCAT-seq: A resin-bead based microfluidic method for both ATAC- and RNA-seq from the same single cell - while this method was very innovative, relatively low sensitivity in both RNA and ATAC coupled with the difficulty of performing Drop-seq/DrONC-seq [check dronc] precluded its inclusion.
    \item ISSAAC-seq \& SHARE-seq: two additional scRNA + scATAC-seq combination methods, one droplet-based and the other combinatorial indexing based. both of these methods were published [late?]
\end{itemize}

At this point, [x] institutes were on board with our benchmark ([list]), and generated a total of 16 datasets across these 7 protocols. Initial reviewers unanimously agreed that we had too few replicates to draw significant conclusions from these datasets. Moreover, 10x Genomics had just released a version 2 of their scATAC-seq method, promising a 50\% increase in sensitivity. We decided to include this method and generate more technical replicates on the initial 7 techniques, yielding a total of 47 datasets across 8 methods (\autoref{fig:scatac_seq_benchmark_study_final_datasets}). Each dataset was sequenced to sufficient depth, and the resulting data was transferred to VIB for analysis.

\section{Analysis}
Due to the large scale and collaborative nature of the project, sequencing data trickled in on an unpredictable basis. We quickly realised that data stemming from the 8 different techniques strongly differed in format. The 10x Genomics format was the most straightforward to work with: two paired-end genomic reads, a sample index read, and a 16 bp barcode read with a whitelist containing ~2.7M entries. HyDrop followed a similar scheme, with index and barcode read switched in position, and a barcode length of 50 bp.
